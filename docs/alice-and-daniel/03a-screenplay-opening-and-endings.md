# What the Water Keeps — screenplay, chapters 1, 6 and 7
Version 1.0 · Authored first draft · Companion: [chapters 2–5](03b-screenplay-chapters-2-5.md)

All principal dialogue for these scenes is written below. Direction describes playable action rather than extra narration. The separate game design specifies puzzle solutions and assistance. Scene IDs are stable; local choices reconverge. Bracketed stage directions are not spoken. This is a script for production development, not a claim that performances, timing, or animation have been tested.

## Chapter 1 — The First Stone

### C1S1 — A sound after its source
Present night. Daniel, thirty-four. Dock and boathouse. The camera looks toward the shore.

A work light illuminates a small part of the lake. Beyond it: houses, one late bus, an ordinary night. Daniel lowers a receiver to the water. His notebook is open on a dry crate.

On the page: numbered tests, several crossed-out measurements, and a shopping list in a different corner.

The player positions the receiver against two visible guide marks. A shop bell rings once from a small test recording. Its original playback stops. From the water, the same bell rings again.

Daniel waits.

DANIEL: Again.

The player can inspect the stable waveform or let it go. Either reveals that the receiver's source recording is no longer running. A second echo sounds at precisely the same interval.

Daniel writes a small tick. He looks at it, then crosses out the tick and writes the actual interval.

His phone vibrates.

JONAH, message: You still need that clamp?

DANIEL, reply: Yes.

JONAH: Then stop moving the bench. I measured it.

The player closes the phone. Daniel rests his hand on the receiver's return control. The echo ends. Ordinary water moves against the dock.

On the facing notebook page is a diagram of three unequal pulses. The receiver briefly produces them: two near each other, the third farther away.

Cut on the third pulse to daylight.

### C1S2 — The wet plank
First summer. Daniel and Alice, thirteen. Same camera position, lower to their height.

A girl sits at the dock's end. Her sleeve is wet. Tears have reached her chin, but she makes no effort to wipe them. Her gaze follows something below the water's surface.

Within her reflection, only for a moment: a vertical work light where there should be sky, the three pulses, the edge of an older man's face. No complete future scene is shown.

Daniel approaches carrying a cloth bag of shop odds and ends. He stops before she notices him.

He picks up a flat stone. One throw. It skips across the image and breaks it apart.

ALICE: You missed.

DANIEL: I wasn't throwing it at you.

ALICE: Good.

She wipes her face with the already-wet sleeve, becomes annoyed with it, and pushes it up her arm.

DANIEL: There's a bench further up.

ALICE: Is that where I'm supposed to sit?

DANIEL: No. It's just dry.

Alice looks down at the plank.

ALICE: Bit late for that.

The player sits beside her or remains by the mooring post.

If seated:

DANIEL: This bit's dry.

ALICE: Congratulations.

If standing:

ALICE: You can sit down.

DANIEL: I know.

ALICE: All right.

Both return to:

She notices the bag.

ALICE: Is that rubbish?

DANIEL: Some of it.

ALICE: Why are you carrying it around?

DANIEL: I haven't found out which bits yet.

A laugh gets out before she can decide whether to give it to him.

A small radio protrudes from her own bag. She turns it so the cracked back faces away from him.

DANIEL: Does that work?

ALICE: Sometimes.

DANIEL: That means no.

ALICE: It means sometimes.

He points up the path, toward the repair shop.

DANIEL: My mum might have a look.

ALICE: Is she expensive?

DANIEL: Depends if she likes you.

ALICE: How much does she charge you?

DANIEL: Quite a lot.

Alice stands. The loose plank tilts under her foot; Daniel steadies its end with his shoe. She steps across without taking his hand.

ALICE: I'm Alice.

DANIEL: Daniel.

ALICE: I moved in on Tuesday.

DANIEL: I know.

She looks at him.

DANIEL: The van blocked the bus.

ALICE: That was us.

Before leaving, she glances once toward the disturbed water. There is no vision to return to. She follows him uphill.

### C1S3 — The shop can wait
First summer. Repair shop. Ruth is working at the counter.

RUTH: If that's the same toaster, I'm keeping it.

DANIEL: It's a radio.

Ruth looks up at Alice.

RUTH: Better company, then.

ALICE: It keeps stopping.

RUTH: Daniel does that too. Usually when I ask him to sweep.

DANIEL: I swept.

RUTH: Around things.

She gives Alice a place to put the radio and slides a tray of safe, familiar spare parts toward Daniel.

P01 begins: inspect the labeled symptoms and match the ordinary parts to the fault. Ruth gives the first hint if requested. The player does not manipulate live mains equipment.

First failed selection:

RUTH: Look at what it's doing before you start replacing everything.

Second hint:

RUTH: The speaker's making a noise. Start with the bit that keeps losing contact.

On completion, the radio catches a cheerful weather report mid-sentence. Alice turns it down.

ALICE: It never does that at home.

RUTH: Things like an audience.

DANIEL: You said that wasn't a diagnosis.

RUTH: It isn't. Write down what you changed.

He enters the repair into his ledger. Alice leans over.

ALICE: Are you writing my name?

DANIEL: For the radio.

ALICE: It isn't called Alice.

DANIEL: What's it called?

ALICE: Radio.

Ruth laughs without looking up.

ALICE: What do I owe you?

RUTH: Bring it back if it stops.

ALICE: That's not a price.

RUTH: No. That's a warning.

Ruth opens a counter drawer. It sticks. She makes a small movement beneath the lip and it slides free. Neither child notices the exact movement.

RUTH: One day I'm fixing that.

DANIEL: You said that last week.

RUTH: And look how close we are to one day.

Alice tests the radio again. Daniel silently moves a hot mug away from its edge. She notices. It is a small kindness with no response required.

Outside, the wind lifts one corner of a notice attached to the shop door.

### C1S4 — Under the counter
Present night. Same noticeboard, newly painted over. Daniel stands on the dock facing the shop's open loading doors.

The component he needs is visible through the jammed cabinet's inspection gap. Pulling normally does not open it. Its fittings have been covered by a later counter repair.

DANIEL: Of course.

P02: the player aligns the lake's reflection of the loading doors with their summer position. Ruth appears in an old closing routine. She reaches under the counter, releases the catch, and opens the drawer.

This is passive playback. She never looks at present Daniel or answers him. The player releases the view, walks into the real shop, and finds the same catch under the later panel.

DANIEL: I moved this thing twice.

The drawer opens. Inside: the damping clamp, a dead pen, and an envelope labeled RETURN TO CUSTOMER. Daniel takes the clamp. He puts the envelope on the counter, where it can be dealt with.

Optional inspect, dead pen:

DANIEL: Not even one of the good ones.

He closes the drawer. The catch sticks again. He leaves it slightly open.

Transition: its sliding sound becomes the transport noise of a recorder.

## Chapter 6 — What the Water Kept

### C6S1 — The north room
Present night observing the last morning. Shore beneath the shop. Alice, twenty-two, appears in a passive trace.

The source has stabilized enough to show a continuous event. The player adjusts viewpoint, but cannot speak to her or change what she does.

Alice records the shop loading doors, then the water, then a bus turning at the end of the street. She checks a level and frowns.

ALICE: That's not wind. That's you.

The distant bus disappears. She waits for a cleaner take.

She records a practical note to herself.

ALICE: Flat. Ask about the north room. Don't say it's fine before you've heard it with the window open.

She stops, considers, starts recording again.

ALICE: And ask Daniel about the table. He doesn't have to like that one.

She puts the recorder away. Her phone lights up; she reads a message and smiles. We do not zoom in on private text as though the player has earned unrestricted access.

She steps off the wet landing. The trace reaches its end.

DANIEL: I liked the table.

He says it to a place where no one can answer.

The player releases the image. The last-morning anchor is now identified. Daniel's ledger labels it MORNING, then adds BEFORE LUNCH.

No blood, warning symptom, or mysterious interruption appears in this scene.

### C6S2 — Preparations
Present night. Boathouse, loading bay, and the real shallows.

The apparatus is materially understandable in outline: three shoreline receivers, a central carrier, and a large return control kept within reach. It is assembled from years of practical work, not a spotless futuristic capsule.

The player verifies the three already-established operations on a simple labeled diagram: VIEW / LOCK / CLOSE. A short optional recap uses illustrations and plain text.

VIEW: Live encounter. Return remains possible while the connection is stable.  
LOCK: Bind awareness into a recorded interval. The interval repeats; the outside life does not resume.  
CLOSE: End access at the source. No new apparatus can restore it.

These are interface explanations, not a long speech from Daniel.

He bends to secure the last housing. A corroded bracket catches his shirt at the left side. He recoils, looks at the scrape, and swears once under his breath.

DANIEL: Brilliant.

He switches the assembly off, cleans and dresses the small injury from the shop kit. The staging is brief and non-graphic. No player medical procedure is required.

His phone contains Jonah's message.

JONAH: Eight. Answer even if it didn't work.

Daniel types: I will.

The player may send “I will” or change it to “Thanks.” Either is an expression, not an ending lock.

He sets the phone on the dry bench, facing up.

Optional inspect, packed food:

DANIEL: After.

He notices his own word. He puts the food where he will see it on returning.

He closes the tool case and tests the return control with an empty link. A small vibration from the shore receiver is felt at his wrist a moment late. He releases the link; sensation returns immediately. His hand does not touch the outline of the test image, though their positions almost coincide.

He enters the shallows and sits in the supported working position. The return control is clearly reachable. On-screen help can remind the player that pausing or taking time to read never advances bodily danger.

### C6S3 — Three shores
Present night. P12, final alignment.

The player aligns the dock, tree, and rise anchors gathered in P11. The challenge combines established observation and matching, with sequential alternatives for every gesture.

Each successful alignment briefly reveals a familiar place at a different age. Nobody addresses Daniel. These are environmental layers, not a chorus of reassuring ghosts.

The current receiver pulses. The first two arrive together; the third follows later. It is the sound from the opening and the childhood glimpse.

The lake's surface becomes a vertical depth while its shoreline remains physically where it was. Reflected structures occupy successive layers: the new counter, the old shopfront, the empty bus shelter, the shelter with its old advertisement.

The score expands here for the first time in several scenes. It carries no choir singing words.

Daniel finds the last-morning anchor. Within it, Alice looks up from her recorder. The player verifies the identity marker against that visit, then checks the return layer against the present work light and Daniel's current position. Both are sequential confirmation steps with the same illustrated assistance as earlier tests. The connection cannot open on a wrongly matched identity or without the return route verified.

He releases the final alignment into stable contact. The music stops at the small sound of her taking a breath.

ALICE: Daniel?

This response is new. It did not occur in the passive trace.

### C6S4 — An answer he did not record
The encounter. A shallow margin of water beside the dock. Daniel is thirty-four; Alice is twenty-two.

ALICE: What's happened to you?

DANIEL: I got older.

ALICE: I can see that.

She looks at his hands, his face, the apparatus behind him.

ALICE: How much?

DANIEL: Twelve years.

She starts to smile because she thinks this might be an appalling joke. His expression prevents it.

ALICE: For you?

DANIEL: Yes.

A long silence. The player can wait indefinitely.

DANIEL: What's the last thing you remember?

ALICE: The bus ruining a take. You wanted me back for lunch.

DANIEL: You came back.

ALICE: Right.

He cannot continue immediately.

ALICE: Daniel.

DANIEL: Your heart stopped.

She looks away. A small ordinary swallow. There is no swell of music and no instant serene understanding.

ALICE: At lunch?

DANIEL: Yes.

ALICE: Was Mara there?

DANIEL: After.

ALICE: And you?

DANIEL: I was there.

ALICE: All right.

She sits. He starts to move beside her, then stops at the water between them.

ALICE: I don't know what to say.

DANIEL: Neither did I.

ALICE: You've had longer.

A short laugh escapes him. It hurts, but it is a laugh.

The player can ask about a shared memory or what she was going to do next.

Shared-memory option:

DANIEL: Do you remember the fundraiser?

ALICE: Unfortunately.

DANIEL: You liked that recording.

ALICE: I liked getting paid.

DANIEL: You didn't get paid.

ALICE: Then I liked the sandwiches.

Future-plan option:

DANIEL: You were going to ask me about the table.

ALICE: I was hoping you'd say you hated it.

DANIEL: I didn't.

ALICE: That's inconvenient.

Both return to:

ALICE: Mara's going to say I should've told you about the job sooner.

DANIEL: She already did.

ALICE: Of course she did.

DANIEL: Did you tell her first?

ALICE: She was there.

ALICE: At the bus shelter. I thought I had to change twice. She pointed out I was reading the arrival platform.

DANIEL: You never told me that.

ALICE: I was trying to look like someone who could get to a job.

Daniel realizes there is something here his notebooks could not have provided. The player has seen the earlier event independently. No interface declares a successful authenticity test.

He reaches toward Alice's hand. Their fingers occupy adjacent layers and do not meet. She tries from her side, then lowers her hand.

ALICE: Oh.

DANIEL: I can hear you.

ALICE: Yes.

DANIEL: That's more than I had.

ALICE: I know.

Neither calls this enough.

## Chapter 7 — Morning

### C7S1 — Things to ask
The encounter continues. No new location.

DANIEL: I had things to ask you.

ALICE: I know.

DANIEL: I wrote them down.

ALICE: Of course you did.

He produces the damp outline of his notebook, then closes it.

DANIEL: Were you coming back?

ALICE: From the job?

He nods.

ALICE: For the weekend.

DANIEL: I mean—

ALICE: I know what you mean.

She takes time.

ALICE: I wanted the work. And I wanted you. I was hoping we'd be less stupid about it the second time.

DANIEL: We were doing better.

ALICE: Good.

This is not a guarantee of a perfect future. She has answered what she actually knew.

DANIEL: I could go back. Come again.

ALICE: Would I remember this?

DANIEL: No. It reads the same morning. What we say here doesn't write back.

She considers the meaning before responding.

ALICE: So you'd have to tell me again.

DANIEL: Yes.

ALICE: I don't want that.

He nods slowly.

ALICE: This time, I know you're here. I don't want to keep finding out where I've been.

DANIEL: If I close it, all the records go. Mum. The summers. Everything in here.

ALICE: Are they waiting?

DANIEL: No. Nothing's awake until we open it.

ALICE: Then let this be the time we had.

She is expressing a preference about her own continued activation. Daniel's later act also sacrifices a shared archive; the story does not pretend that loss belongs only to him or that there is a cost-free answer.

The player chooses one of three remaining subjects. After the response, all remain available; “Sit quietly” advances after at least one subject, or immediately in accessibility story mode. None changes the ending set.

**Subject: Ruth**

DANIEL: Mum retired.

ALICE: Did she?

DANIEL: For nearly two years.

ALICE: What did she do?

DANIEL: Complained about other people's repairs.

Alice laughs. Daniel smiles, then lets it fade.

DANIEL: She's gone too.

ALICE: I'm sorry.

DANIEL: She liked you.

ALICE: She let me owe her money.

DANIEL: She never wrote it down.

**Subject: Mara**

DANIEL: I gave Mara the recorder.

ALICE: Her recorder?

DANIEL: Yes.

ALICE: How long did that take?

He looks at her.

ALICE: Right.

A beat.

ALICE: She'll be pleased.

DANIEL: She was mostly cross.

ALICE: She can do both.

**Subject: Jonah**

DANIEL: Jonah's got a child.

ALICE: That's ambitious.

DANIEL: He says the same thing about me.

ALICE: Sensible child?

DANIEL: I haven't seen them enough.

ALICE: Oh.

She leaves the answer there. No instruction follows.

**On choosing quiet**

Their attention settles on the dock.

ALICE: I think I've been here.

DANIEL: We were here all the time.

ALICE: Like this.

The three pulses reach them faintly. Alice looks toward the place where her younger self sat.

ALICE: There was a light in the water. I couldn't understand where it was coming from.

Daniel looks back at the work light.

DANIEL: The day we met.

ALICE: You threw something.

DANIEL: A stone.

ALICE: I remember the splash.

He starts to ask another question and stops.

The camera returns for one short shot to thirteen-year-old Alice looking into the opening reflection. The older face, light, and pulse occupy the positions now established. The shot ends before the stone breaks it.

Return to the present encounter.

ALICE: I thought I was crying about moving.

DANIEL: Were you?

ALICE: Probably that too.

This prevents the revelation from consuming her entire ordinary life.

### C7S2 — Turn around
The encounter. The last shared sequence before any branch.

DANIEL: I thought if I got this right—

ALICE: You did.

He shakes his head, not disputing the machine's success.

DANIEL: I don't know what to do with it.

She studies him, then the shore behind him.

ALICE: Turn around.

The player turns the view. A simple press and an automatic accessible equivalent both work.

Three shots. No overlays. No character voices. No apparitions.

The plank at the dock's end. Its damaged edge has been repaired, poorly.

The measuring tree. The old marks have spread with the bark.

The rise. A low fence has replaced part of the path where Ruth used to stand.

Return to the same present framing. Nothing has moved. The work light buzzes once and steadies.

Daniel looks at Alice.

DANIEL: Goodbye, Alice.

ALICE: Goodbye, Daniel.

Neither leaves. The farewell exists in every branch and is the event the opening vision contained.

Then the water catches a different season. A bright afternoon. Ruth's shop doors are open. Jonah crosses behind them carrying too much at once. Alice is twenty-one.

A phrase returns in Daniel's own familiar voice, taken from earlier moments of play and testing:

DANIEL'S RECORDED VOICE: Just one more.

No new speaker identifies itself as the lake.

### C7S3 — The offer
The water offers a short preview of the last complete summer before her death.

A glass falls. Jonah says the same word. Alice looks up at the same sound.

The moment returns to its beginning. A full summer is available beyond it, but its boundary is visible on Daniel's already-established interval display. The end folds to the beginning before autumn.

DANIEL: It goes round.

ALICE: And you remember?

DANIEL: Yes.

She watches the same small accident for a third time.

ALICE: I wouldn't know what you'd already heard.

He does not answer.

The live return indicator remains visible. A minor lag passes through Daniel's hand. The exit route is still available.

ALICE: Can you get back?

DANIEL: Yes.

ALICE: Now?

DANIEL: Now.

The player selects an intent. Three equally legible options appear with plain consequence text; no moral colors or rank labels.

**Take the summer.** Bind Daniel's awareness to the repeating interval. He remembers every cycle and does not return to life outside it.

**Stay in this encounter.** Decline the loop and keep the live connection open beyond safe return. Daniel will die in the real water.

**Return and close the lake.** Regain bodily control and permanently end the source. Alice remains dead; further encounters end.

Each selection opens a confirm/cancel step. Cancel restores all choices. The game can be paused or closed here without advancing danger. Confirmation commits the branch atomically, then creates a separate replay checkpoint.

Alice does not endorse a menu selection before it happens.

### C7S4 / E1 — One More Summer

Daniel selects the interval lock.

DANIEL: I want the summer.

ALICE: I heard you.

DANIEL: I know what it does.

She looks toward the bright version of herself.

ALICE: I hope—

She stops. No blessing can make the choice something else.

ALICE: I don't know what to say.

DANIEL: You don't have to.

He turns the lock control. The responsive encounter dissolves into the chosen interval. Alice's new knowledge of this farewell does not follow the fixed replay.

Daylight. Daniel is twenty-one. Ruth calls from the shop. Jonah has bought the wrong drinks. Alice is trying to free her sleeve from a gate latch.

ALICE: A hand would be useful.

Daniel's recorded younger body helps her. Their contact is warm and ordinary. His older awareness receives it.

The first passage through selected days is beautiful without being perfect: a missed bus, an argument over borrowed equipment, Alice falling asleep before a recording finishes, Ruth refusing to open on a Sunday.

The summer reaches its boundary.

Cut to daylight. The same angle.

ALICE: A hand would be useful.

Daniel's recorded body moves. His awareness has already anticipated the line.

The glass falls.

JONAH: Oh, come on.

At the next iteration, the player may press to reach for it. The gesture registers as Daniel's intention, but the fixed body's motion follows its recorded path. This limitation was previewed before the decision; it is not a surprise punishment.

The glass falls.

On a later afternoon, Alice turns to him.

ALICE: What are you looking at?

His recorded younger self answers.

YOUNG DANIEL: You.

The player hears the answer for the third time. His eyes cannot carry the years nobody else remembers.

The camera stays in the warm afternoon long enough for its continuation to become uncomfortable. Fade before a visible counter makes eternity into a score.

End title: ONE MORE SUMMER.

A quiet credits screen offers return to the pre-choice checkpoint. No celebratory achievement appears.

### C7S4 / E2 — Until the Water Stills

Daniel dismisses the fixed summer. The live encounter remains.

DANIEL: Not that.

ALICE: Then go back.

DANIEL: A little longer.

She looks at the return indicator, then him.

ALICE: Daniel.

DANIEL: I heard you.

His previously confirmed choice now takes effect; this is an authored action, not a second decision after commitment. There is no button-mashing struggle or countdown.

The ordinary shore begins to arrive in the wrong places: the work light through a reflected tree, cold against his palm, a tremor that belongs to his body elsewhere.

The small mark at his side darkens. Alice sees it.

ALICE: Is that from now?

DANIEL: Before I came in.

She looks toward the real position of his body. The separation is failing.

Without panic or a speech, she steps into the shallows. Her first attempt to take his hand passes through. She adjusts to the place where his sensation has arrived, and this time their fingers meet.

This is the first physical contact of this encounter. It carries no new promise about what follows death.

ALICE: There.

He looks down at their hands.

DANIEL: I can feel it.

ALICE: I know.

A quiet pause.

DANIEL: Do you remember that radio?

ALICE: Which time it broke?

DANIEL: The first.

ALICE: You wrote my name down.

DANIEL: For the radio.

ALICE: It wasn't called Alice.

He almost laughs.

DANIEL: Radio.

She smiles.

DANIEL: I liked walking back with you.

ALICE: I know.

DANIEL: I kept going past my house.

ALICE: Your house was above the shop.

DANIEL: I wasn't very good at it.

ALICE: No.

She keeps his hand. The water's tiny movements cease one by one. The scene does not show a romantic underwater embrace or lead into a shared afterlife.

Cut to morning outside the encounter. Jonah's unanswered call lights the phone on the dry bench. He arrives along the path. At the boathouse, he stops.

JONAH: Daniel?

Cut to a static exterior. Jonah calls emergency help out of frame; words are not distinct. Responders arrive. The camera does not search for the body.

The notebook lies on the dry crate. It is still open to the test measurements.

End title: UNTIL THE WATER STILLS.

Reduced-intensity mode cuts from joined hands directly to the static exterior and retains the same comprehension.

### C7S4 / E3 — Morning

Daniel chooses return and closure.

He rests his hand above the return control. There is no urgent sound. They have already said goodbye in the shared scene. He lets that farewell stand.

He looks at the far end of the dock, the place from which a boy once expected to spend another afternoon.

DANIEL: And you. Goodbye.

No child appears. Alice does not explain whom he means.

DANIEL: I was thirteen.

ALICE: So was I.

For a moment, both find this difficult to reconcile with the work light and his older face.

DANIEL: I'm glad you came.

ALICE: Me too.

She notices his notebook.

ALICE: For the record, it was fourteen metres twenty.

DANIEL: I wrote that.

ALICE: You were right about one thing, then.

They share the small laugh from the first summer. She makes the little upward motion of her chin that used to mean he could stop waiting for her. There is tenderness here without physical contact or a promise that she will remain nearby.

He releases the live connection.

Daniel stands in real water. The familiar source remains aligned; the closure control is the same operation learned through the washer trace. The player activates it with one deliberate action, or an equivalent accessible selection. This action cannot fail.

The lake's impossible depth collapses toward its surface. The three reflected eras cease to coincide. Past structures disappear without being blown apart. The stored voice cuts off between two ordinary background sounds.

The real shop remains. The real trees remain. Water moves against the support under Daniel's hand.

He disconnects the central core and breaks its fragile carrier plate. This is a small physical action after the larger irreversible loss. He leaves no active apparatus in the water.

He reaches the shore.

The walk home is playable in three short sections. The plank, tree, and rise retain their empty compositions. Environmental sounds continue; nobody whispers goodbye. The player may stop at each place without triggering an extra memory.

At the flat, Daniel removes the wet shirt, checks the dressing, and puts dry clothes on. He takes the food from his bag and finally eats some of it.

His phone shows the message from Jonah.

Daniel writes: Breakfast. If you're free.

Jonah replies after a brief, non-blocking interval.

JONAH: Eight?

DANIEL: Later.

JONAH: Good.

Mara's separate message asks where she left the recorder case. Daniel replies: Counter. I'll bring it.

These contacts do not become a reunion ceremony.

At the table, the player opens the ledger. Measurements occupy the last page. Daniel turns to a new one.

He writes three small observations: the missing corner of the dock plank; the laugh retained on a bad recording; his mother's handwriting on an unpaid envelope.

He does not list every detail of Alice's face to stop himself forgetting.

Daniel closes the unfinished entry and goes to bed. The camera remains outside the bedroom. Night ends.

Morning. A brief playable coda in the kitchen.

He sets out two cups without thinking. Notices. Puts one back.

There is no musical instruction about how to feel.

He makes tea, picks up Mara's case, and leaves for breakfast. The ledger stays on the table, closed but within reach.

The last shot is the kitchen after he has left it. Through the open window: a bus door, ordinary voices, a day already underway.

Over that shot, a later ledger entry appears at normal reading speed, explicitly labeled “Later entry.” Its tense is retrospective, not a prophecy written before he slept. No handwriting tracing is required:

“The water remembered me first — but I remembered them longest, and then I went to bed, and then, in the morning, I went on.”

End title: MORNING.

## Global optional interaction lines

These are the complete reusable lines for inspectable transitions in this script. Silence is preferable to extra chatter. Never repeat a line on rapid repeated taps.

- Present receiver not aligned: DANIEL: “Not there.”
- Successful ordinary correction: DANIEL: “There.”
- Unnecessary repeat of a stable test: DANIEL: “It's the same result.”
- Present shop threshold: DANIEL: “Mind the step.” He realizes nobody is behind him; no follow-up.
- Childhood unsuitable stone: ALICE: “That's a potato.”
- Childhood ledger inspection: ALICE: “You can leave that bit out.” DANIEL: “Which bit?” ALICE: “Me losing.”
- Reunion return-control inspect: DANIEL: “That takes me back.”
- Reunion closure-control inspect: DANIEL: “There won't be another.”
- Menu recap and accessibility prompts use neutral interface text, never a character scolding the player.
