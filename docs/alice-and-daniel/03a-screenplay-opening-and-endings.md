# What the Water Keeps — screenplay, chapters 1, 6 and 7
Version 1.2 · Authored development draft · Companion: [chapters 2–5](03b-screenplay-chapters-2-5.md)

All principal dialogue for these scenes is written below. Direction describes playable action rather than extra narration. The separate game design specifies puzzle solutions and assistance. Scene IDs are stable; local choices reconverge. Bracketed stage directions are not spoken. This is a script for production development, not a claim that performances, timing, or animation have been tested.

## Chapter 1 — The First Stone

### C1S1 — A sound after its source
Present night. Daniel, thirty-four. Dock and boathouse. The camera looks toward the shore.

A work light illuminates a small part of the lake. Beyond it: houses, one late bus, an ordinary night. Daniel lowers a receiver to the water. His notebook is open on a dry crate.

On the page: numbered tests, several crossed-out measurements, and a shopping list in a different corner. His thumb holds the place while he adjusts the receiver. The page can be readable without every measurement becoming a collectible.

The player positions the receiver against two visible guide marks. A shop bell rings once from a small test recording. Its original playback stops. From the water, the same bell rings again.

Daniel waits.

DANIEL: Again.

The player can inspect the stable waveform or let it go. Either reveals that the receiver's source recording is no longer running. A second echo sounds at precisely the same interval.

Daniel writes a small tick. He looks at it, then crosses out the tick and writes the actual interval.

His phone vibrates.

JONAH, message: You still need that clamp?

DANIEL, reply: Yes.

JONAH: Then stop moving the bench. I measured it.

The player closes the phone. Daniel rests his hand on the receiver's return control. The echo ends. Ordinary water moves against the dock.

On the facing notebook page is a diagram of three unequal pulses. The receiver briefly produces them: two near each other, the third farther away.

Cut on the third pulse to daylight.

### C1S2 — The wet plank
First summer. Daniel and Alice, thirteen. Same camera position, lower to their height.

A girl sits at the dock's end. Her sleeve is wet. Tears have reached her chin, but she makes no effort to wipe them. Her gaze follows something below the water's surface.

Within her reflection, only for a moment: a vertical work light where there should be sky, the three pulses, the edge of an older man's face. No complete future scene is shown.

Daniel approaches carrying a cloth bag of shop odds and ends. He stops before she notices him.

He picks up a flat stone. One throw. It skips across the image and breaks it apart.

ALICE: You missed.

DANIEL: I wasn't throwing it at you.

ALICE: Good.

She wipes her face with the already-wet sleeve, becomes annoyed with it, and pushes it up her arm.

DANIEL: There's a bench further up.

ALICE: Is that where I'm supposed to sit?

DANIEL: No. It's just dry.

Alice looks down at the plank.

ALICE: Bit late for that.

The player sits beside her or remains by the mooring post.

If seated:

DANIEL: This bit's dry.

ALICE: Congratulations.

If standing:

ALICE: You can sit down.

DANIEL: I know.

ALICE: All right.

Both return to:

She notices the bag.

ALICE: Is that rubbish?

DANIEL: Some of it.

ALICE: Why are you carrying it around?

DANIEL: I haven't found out which bits yet.

A laugh gets out before she can decide whether to give it to him.

A small radio protrudes from her own bag. She turns it so the cracked back faces away from him.

DANIEL: Does that work?

ALICE: Sometimes.

DANIEL: That means no.

ALICE: It means sometimes.

He points up the path, toward the repair shop.

DANIEL: My mum might have a look.

ALICE: Is she expensive?

DANIEL: Depends if she likes you.

ALICE: How much does she charge you?

DANIEL: Quite a lot.

Alice stands. The loose plank tilts under her foot; Daniel steadies its end with his shoe. She steps across without taking his hand.

ALICE: I'm Alice.

DANIEL: Daniel.

ALICE: I moved in on Tuesday.

DANIEL: I know.

She looks at him.

DANIEL: The van blocked the bus.

ALICE: That was us.

Before leaving, she glances once toward the disturbed water. There is no vision to return to. She follows him uphill.

### C1S3 — The shop can wait
First summer. Repair shop. Ruth is working at the counter.

RUTH: If that's the same toaster, I'm keeping it.

DANIEL: It's a radio.

Ruth looks up at Alice.

RUTH: Better company, then.

ALICE: It keeps stopping.

Ruth holds out a hand for the small screwdriver. Daniel gives her the wrong one, notices, and swaps it before she has to ask.

RUTH: Thanks. Alice, is it all right if we open the back?

ALICE: Yes.

She gives Alice a place to put the radio and slides a tray of safe, familiar spare parts toward Daniel.

P01 begins with the concrete aim **Keep the radio playing when it is moved**. Inspect the battery charge, speaker output, and what changes when the casing moves; the raw observations do not name the faulty part. Select a labeled ordinary part from the tray and test it. A battery or speaker change is reversible and leaves the same interruption; replacing the contact makes the test hold. Give clear evidence after each test rather than a guessed-answer penalty. Ruth speaks the following hints only on request; the player does not manipulate live mains equipment.

First requested hint:

RUTH: What changes when you move it?

Second requested hint:

RUTH: The battery's charged. Look at the contact that should keep it connected.

On completion, the interrupted weather sentence reaches its end. Alice turns the radio down.

ALICE: It never does that at home.

RUTH: Try tilting it now.

Alice tilts the radio the same way that interrupted it before. The weather report continues. Daniel looks pleased.

RUTH: Good. Write down what you changed.

He enters the repair into his ledger. Alice leans over.

ALICE: Are you writing my name?

DANIEL: For the radio.

ALICE: It isn't called Alice.

DANIEL: What's it called?

ALICE: Radio.

Ruth laughs without looking up.

ALICE: What do I owe you?

RUTH: Bring it back if it stops.

ALICE: That's not a price.

RUTH: No. That's a warning.

Ruth opens a counter drawer. It sticks. She makes a small movement beneath the lip and it slides free. Neither child notices the exact movement.

RUTH: One day I'm fixing that.

She puts her tea down and goes straight back to the repair waiting beside it.

Alice tests the radio again. Daniel silently moves a hot mug away from its edge. She notices. It is a small kindness with no response required.

Outside, the wind lifts one corner of a notice attached to the shop door.

### C1S4 — Under the counter
Present night. Same noticeboard, newly painted over. Daniel stands on the dock facing the shop's open loading doors.

The component he needs is visible through the jammed cabinet's inspection gap. Pulling normally does not open it. Its fittings have been covered by a later counter repair.

DANIEL: Of course.

P02: the obstructed cabinet and visible clamp establish the problem before the solution is shown. The player can inspect the present handle and lower panel seam, then align the lake's reflection of the loading doors with their summer position. Ruth appears in an old closing routine. She reaches under the counter, releases the catch, and opens the drawer.

This is passive playback. She never looks at present Daniel or answers him. Its factual event log describes her hand going under the lower lip and the catch moving before the drawer opens; it does not automatically point to the next present hotspot. The player releases the view and applies the observation beneath the later panel. Return, evidence review, and replay remain freely available. Prior inspection of the handle does not waste an attempt; once the observation is acquired, it need not be repeated to act.

DANIEL: I moved this thing twice.

The drawer opens. Inside: the damping clamp, a dead pen, and an envelope labeled RETURN TO CUSTOMER. Daniel takes the clamp. He puts the envelope on the counter, where it can be dealt with.

Beside the counter, a repaired hall amplifier has a completed service tag and a phone number still waiting for a reply. Keep it a normal workshop object. Its meaning will come from Jonah's later complaint, not an ominous close-up or a guilt meter.

Optional inspect, dead pen:

DANIEL: Not even one of the good ones.

He closes the drawer. The catch sticks again. He leaves it slightly open.

Transition: its sliding sound becomes the transport noise of a recorder.

## Chapter 6 — What the Water Kept

### C6S1 — The north room
Present night observing the last morning. Shore beneath the shop. Alice, twenty-two, appears in a passive trace.

The source has stabilized enough to show a continuous event. The player adjusts viewpoint, but cannot speak to her or change what she does.

Alice records the shop loading doors, then the water, then a bus turning at the end of the street. She checks a level and frowns.

ALICE [quiet slate into recorder]: Bus. Start again after.

The distant bus disappears. She waits for a cleaner take.

She records a practical note to herself.

ALICE: Flat. Ask about the north room. Don't say it's fine before you've heard it with the window open.

She stops, considers, starts recording again.

ALICE: And ask Daniel about the table. He doesn't have to like that one.

She puts the recorder away. Her phone lights up; she reads a message and smiles. We do not zoom in on private text as though the player has earned unrestricted access.

She steps off the wet landing. The trace reaches its end.

DANIEL: I liked the table.

He says it to a place where no one can answer.

The player releases the image. The last-morning anchor is now identified. Daniel's ledger labels it MORNING, then adds BEFORE LUNCH.

No blood, warning symptom, or mysterious interruption appears in this scene.

### C6S2 — Preparations
Present night. Boathouse, loading bay, and the real shallows.

The apparatus is materially understandable in outline: three shoreline receivers, a central carrier, and a large return control kept within reach. It is assembled from years of practical work, not a spotless futuristic capsule.

The player verifies the three already-established operations on a simple labeled diagram: VIEW / LOCK / CLOSE. A short optional recap uses illustrations and plain text.

VIEW: Live encounter. Return remains possible while the connection is stable.  
LOCK: Bind awareness into a recorded interval. The interval repeats; the outside life does not resume.  
CLOSE: End access at the source. No new apparatus can restore it.

These are interface explanations, not a long speech from Daniel.

He bends to secure the last housing. A corroded bracket catches his shirt at the left side. He recoils, looks at the scrape, and swears once under his breath.

DANIEL: Brilliant.

He switches the assembly off, cleans and dresses the small injury from the shop kit. The staging is brief and non-graphic. No player medical procedure is required.

His phone contains Jonah's message.

JONAH: Eight. Answer even if it didn't work.

Daniel types: I will.

The player may send “I will” or change it to “Thanks.” Either is an expression, not an ending lock.

He sets the phone on the dry bench, facing up.

Optional inspect, packed food:

DANIEL: After.

He notices his own word. He puts the food where he will see it on returning.

He closes the tool case and tests the return control with an empty link. A small vibration from the shore receiver is felt at his wrist a moment late. He releases the link; sensation returns immediately. His hand does not touch the outline of the test image, though their positions almost coincide.

He enters the shallows and sits in the supported working position. The return control is clearly reachable. On-screen help can remind the player that pausing or taking time to read never advances bodily danger.

### C6S3 — Three shores
Present night. P12, final synthesis. Use B03 controls and the established D03 shore composition; the existing bounded reflection shows at most three readable layers. No new landscape, simulation, view, speaking figure, or camera rig is required.

Daniel opens the completed source map. Its three anchors remain solved. The machine asks for the source, the stored person-state, and the live return carrier. These are the three kinds of evidence the player has already used, not three more copies of P11.

**P12: Make contact possible without losing the way back.** The player can inspect these channels in any order. Pair the connected lake source with its channel, select Alice's latest morning shore visit for the identity channel, and match the present return carrier to Daniel's working position and work light. The saved source map is reused directly. A wrong configuration remains unpowered and names the missing or mismatched dependency. The return path is verified before any responsive encounter can open. Inspection does not ask Alice a question or authenticate her personality.

Every successful check changes the existing composition, showing what the player's work has made possible:

**Source ready:** an earlier shopfront becomes visible at depth inside the selected water view. The present wall remains in place above it. The loose fragments now share one continuous edge. Daniel traces it with his gaze, then checks the connected map.

DANIEL: All three.

He says it with pleasure. Years of partial results have just become one usable result. Hold only until the player chooses the next step.

**Identity ready:** the morning shore layer settles. Alice puts away her recorder exactly as she did in C6S1. This is still passive playback; she does not notice Daniel. Its caption continues to read **Recorded event**. The player has found the correct morning, rather than summoned a responsive person early.

**Return ready:** the current work light and Daniel's supported real position remain sharply readable at the edge of the older view. The return status becomes **Available**. No safety route is erased to make the spectacle impressive.

Because channel order is free, each readiness beat plays when its own dependency is solved. The following release beat waits for all three; it does not assume a particular input order. Hints and **Complete calibration** produce the same observable changes.

Daniel checks the completed states: **Contact ready / Return available / No history alteration**. He rests his hand beside the final control without pressing it for the player.

DANIEL: There you are.

He is addressing the stable signal. Alice has not answered him yet.

The player selects **Open contact**. This is P12's existing final release, not a new reflex test.

The receiver produces the familiar three pulses: two close together, the third farther away. The shore remains where it is, but its reflection has depth it should not possess. A summer shopfront, the last-morning landing, and the live work light briefly occupy three distinct planes within that same bounded view. Reuse the established art layers; the rest of the town does not transform.

The existing musical motif expands. Allow a brief crest to register: the player completed the apparatus, and it works. This is earned scale, not a warning that the success was fraudulent.

Then the morning playback reaches the point at which Alice used to leave.

She stops.

The **Recorded event** label changes to **Live encounter**. Do not add an authenticity score.

Music gives way to a small intake of breath. Alice turns toward the older man where no one stood in the recorded morning.

ALICE: Daniel?

End the scene on the new answer. There is no demand for another calibration before they can speak.

### C6S4 — An answer he did not record
The encounter. A shallow margin of water beside the dock. Daniel is thirty-four; Alice is twenty-two. Use the established reunion composition. Their different ages must be readable before anyone explains them.

Alice looks from his face to his hands. Daniel starts with a question he has clearly rehearsed.

DANIEL: What's the last thing you—

ALICE: What's happened to you?

He stops. She is not following his prepared conversation.

DANIEL: I got older.

ALICE: I can see that. How much?

DANIEL: Twelve years.

She almost smiles, as though there must be a joke she has not understood. He does not rescue her with one.

ALICE: For you?

DANIEL: Yes.

A silence. No compulsory long hold. The player advances when ready.

ALICE: I was down here this morning.

DANIEL: I know.

ALICE: The bus ruined a take. You wanted me back for lunch.

DANIEL: You came back.

ALICE: Then tell me.

DANIEL: Your heart stopped.

She looks away. A small ordinary swallow. No score announces the correct response.

ALICE: At lunch?

DANIEL: Yes.

ALICE: Was Mara there?

DANIEL: After.

ALICE: And you?

DANIEL: I was there.

She sits. He looks automatically for somewhere drier, then stops: comfort is not a chair he can fetch from the other layer.

ALICE: Did we get the flat?

DANIEL: No.

Her hand goes to the place where the recorder strap should be.

ALICE: I was meant to send the files.

He lets her finish.

ALICE: That's a stupid thing to say.

DANIEL: No.

ALICE: I can't make it feel like twelve years. I haven't had lunch yet.

Daniel lowers himself to her level, without trying to touch her.

ALICE: Give me a minute.

DANIEL: All right.

The next advance belongs to the player. Waiting, reading, pausing, or using the dialogue history never changes bodily danger. Alice's shock has space; it does not consume a hidden allowance.

On advance, she looks toward the machine and back at Daniel.

ALICE: You built all that?

DANIEL: Not all of it. Jonah fixed the brake.

ALICE: Good.

A small exhalation. It is the first familiar judgment she has been able to make.

ALICE: Tell me something I know.

The player may ask about a shared memory or the plan for the flat. Neither is a quiz, a relationship test, or a branch gate.

**Shared-memory option:**

DANIEL: Do you remember the fundraiser?

ALICE: Which bit?

DANIEL: The mugs.

Her laugh comes before she can explain it. Daniel joins it; for this brief passage both are in the same conversation.

ALICE: He counted them on the floor before he stood up.

DANIEL: I kept the take you used.

ALICE: Good. I was good that day.

DANIEL: You were.

Let her take credit. Do not follow the exchange with a modest denial or another wound.

**Future-plan option:**

DANIEL: You were going to ask me about the table.

ALICE: I was hoping you'd say you hated it.

DANIEL: I didn't.

ALICE: I know. That was the problem.

He laughs. She smiles back, pleased to have found him for a moment.

DANIEL: You could have told me.

ALICE: Apparently I was going to spend breakfast asking about doors instead.

**Both return to:**

ALICE: The first job. Mara knew before you did.

DANIEL: I guessed.

ALICE: I told you I had everything sorted. The room. The travel.

DANIEL: You did.

ALICE: I had two changes written down. One of them was an arrival platform.

He looks at her.

ALICE: Mara had to turn the timetable round for me.

DANIEL: You never told me that.

ALICE: I was hoping you'd think they were employing an adult.

The detail extends the private C4S2 exchange that the player saw without Daniel. It emerges because Alice is trying to understand the life that stopped, not because he requests a secret answer. Her interrupting him, choosing a subject, recalling her own embarrassment, and asking for time also establish an independent person. No interface announces proof of consciousness; the story's fiction already defines her as a conscious continuation.

Daniel reaches toward her hand. His fingers do not meet hers. Alice tries from her side, notices the gap between the layers, and lets her hand fall. Neither repeats the gesture as a dexterity challenge.

ALICE: Oh.

Daniel almost begins a technical explanation. She shakes her head once. She has seen what happened.

She moves her hand to the corresponding place on her side of the dock. He leaves his beside it in his layer.

ALICE: There. I can see you.

They sit together without claiming the distance has vanished. This shared tenderness belongs to every ending; the fatal branch does not own the reunion's warmth.

## Chapter 7 — Morning

### C7S1 — Things to ask
The encounter continues. No new location.

Daniel has the damp outline of his notebook against his knee. Alice notices the pages he has marked.

ALICE: What were you going to ask?

DANIEL: Whether you were looking forward to the flat.

ALICE: Yes.

DANIEL: To all of it?

She studies him before answering.

ALICE: I don't know what you're asking me to promise.

DANIEL: That we'd have managed.

ALICE: We hadn't tried it.

He looks at the notebook, then closes it. No perfect answer is being withheld somewhere in her memory.

ALICE: I wanted the job. I wanted you. I was looking forward to complaining about that room together.

DANIEL: I'd have fixed the window.

ALICE: You'd have asked first.

DANIEL: Yes.

She believes him. This is a small credible change from the packing argument, not a guarantee their unwritten life would have been painless.

DANIEL: I could go back. The records would still be here. I could come again.

ALICE: What happens here when you go?

DANIEL: This stops.

ALICE: Would I remember this?

DANIEL: No. You'd be on the shore. Before lunch. You wouldn't have any of this.

She considers the answer. He does not speak over the silence.

ALICE: I'd ask what happened to you.

DANIEL: Yes.

ALICE: And you'd tell me again.

DANIEL: Yes.

She looks at his face as it is now, the one she has only just learned how to speak to.

ALICE: I don't want that.

DANIEL: All right.

He acknowledges her preference without offering a shorter visit, a more careful explanation, or a memory trick. This conversation cannot be written back into her stored biological person-state.

DANIEL: If I close it, all the records go. Mum. The summers. Everything in here.

ALICE: Not just mine.

DANIEL: No.

ALICE: Are they waiting?

DANIEL: No. Nothing's awake until we open it.

Alice looks toward the place where Ruth used to stand. She cannot give Daniel permission on everyone else's behalf. He must decide about a shared, dormant archive after understanding her separate preference about reactivation. Ordinary recordings survive; no other conscious population is secretly waiting below the surface.

The player chooses one of three remaining subjects. After the response, all remain available; “Sit quietly” advances after at least one subject, or immediately in accessibility story mode. None changes the ending set.

**Subject: Ruth**

DANIEL: Mum retired.

ALICE: Did she?

DANIEL: For nearly two years.

ALICE: What did she do?

DANIEL: Took a coach trip. Sent me a photograph of a broken handrail.

Alice laughs. Daniel smiles, then lets it fade. Use the same ordinary retirement photograph established in C5S3 only if the interface offers an inspection; no new location, image montage, or voice of Ruth enters the encounter.

DANIEL: She's gone too.

ALICE: I'm sorry.

Alice shifts a little closer in her layer. It cannot close the distance, but he notices the effort.

DANIEL: She was good on the trip. She liked it.

ALICE: Good.

**Subject: Mara**

DANIEL: I gave Mara the recorder.

ALICE: Her recorder?

DANIEL: Yes.

ALICE: How long did that take?

He looks at her.

ALICE: Right.

A beat.

ALICE: She'll be pleased.

DANIEL: She was mostly cross.

ALICE: She can do both.

**Subject: Jonah**

DANIEL: Jonah's got a child.

ALICE: How old?

DANIEL: Young. They like the blue cup. I don't even know which birthday we're at.

He stops trying to make that sound like an adequate update.

DANIEL: I haven't seen them enough.

ALICE: Oh.

She leaves the answer there. No instruction follows.

**On choosing quiet**

Their attention settles on the dock. The receiver produces the three unequal pulses at the same low level as the opening glimpse.

Alice turns toward the work light.

ALICE: I remember that.

DANIEL: The sound?

ALICE: The light. Under the water.

DANIEL: I put it up tonight.

She looks toward the plank where she sat as a child.

ALICE: No. Before I knew you.

Daniel follows her gaze. Hold on the existing composition while the relationship between its parts becomes clear; do not introduce a fresh hidden clue.

DANIEL: The day you moved in?

ALICE: A few days after.

DANIEL: Your sleeve was wet.

ALICE: You threw a stone.

The camera repeats the brief childhood view from C1S2. The edge of the older face, the vertical light, and the unequal pulses occupy the positions the player has just seen. Use the same material. Add no glimpse of a loop, fatal hand contact, source erasure, or morning. The impression concerns the common encounter and coming farewell before any ending choice.

Return to the two adults.

DANIEL: You were crying.

ALICE: I couldn't have told you why.

She remembers a fragment now that its context is in front of her, not a complete life she consciously carried since thirteen.

ALICE: I wanted to go back to our old house. That was real too.

DANIEL: I know.

He looks toward the place from which he approached with the bag of broken parts. She watches him work it out. They do not explain the mechanism again or summarize twenty-one years of scenes.

The next shared farewell completes the connection. It does not prove that Daniel's final choice was predestined.

### C7S2 — Turn around
The encounter. The last shared sequence before any branch.

DANIEL: I thought if I got this right—

ALICE: You did.

He shakes his head, not disputing the machine's success.

DANIEL: I don't know what to do with it.

She studies him, then the shore behind him.

ALICE: Turn around.

The player turns the view. A simple press and an automatic accessible equivalent both work.

Three shots. No overlays. No character voices. No apparitions.

The plank at the dock's end. Its damaged edge has been repaired, poorly.

The measuring tree. The old marks have spread with the bark.

The rise. A low fence has replaced part of the path where Ruth used to stand.

Return to the same present framing. Nothing has moved. The work light buzzes once and steadies.

Daniel looks at Alice.

DANIEL: Goodbye, Alice.

ALICE: Goodbye, Daniel.

Neither leaves. The farewell exists in every branch and is the event the opening vision contained.

Then the water catches a different season. A bright afternoon. Ruth's shop doors are open. Jonah crosses behind them carrying too much at once. Alice is twenty-one.

A phrase returns in Daniel's own familiar voice, reusing the exact unaccented recording from the childhood throws in C2S2:

DANIEL'S RECORDED VOICE: Just one more.

No new speaker identifies itself as the lake.

### C7S3 — The offer
The water offers a short preview of the last complete summer before her death.

A glass falls. Jonah says the same word. Alice looks up at the same sound.

The moment returns to its beginning. A full summer is available beyond it, but its boundary is visible on Daniel's already-established interval display. The end folds to the beginning before autumn.

DANIEL: It goes round.

ALICE: And you remember?

DANIEL: Yes.

She watches the same small accident for a third time.

ALICE: I wouldn't know what you'd already heard.

He does not answer.

The live return indicator remains visible. A minor lag passes through Daniel's hand. The exit route is still available.

ALICE: Can you get back?

DANIEL: Yes.

ALICE: Now?

DANIEL: Now.

The player selects an intent. Three equally legible options appear with plain consequence text; no moral colors or rank labels.

**Take the summer.** Bind Daniel's awareness to the last complete summer. Everyone else repeats. He remembers each cycle, cannot change its recorded events, and does not return to life outside it. The responsive encounter ends; Alice's new memories do not enter the loop.

**Stay in this encounter.** Decline the loop and keep the live connection open beyond safe return. Daniel will die in the real water. This does not promise another life together.

**Return and close the lake.** Regain bodily control and permanently erase all of the lake's stored records. Alice remains dead; future activation ends, and another apparatus cannot restore the erased source. Ordinary water and ordinary recordings remain.

Each selection opens a confirm/cancel step. Cancel restores all choices. The game can be paused or closed here without advancing danger. Confirmation durably commits the chosen branch while preserving the pre-choice snapshot for separate ending replay.

Alice does not endorse a menu selection before it happens.

### C7S4 / E1 — One More Summer

Daniel selects the interval lock.

DANIEL: I want the summer.

ALICE: I heard you.

DANIEL: I know what it does.

She looks toward the bright version of herself.

ALICE: I hope—

She stops. No blessing can make the choice something else.

ALICE: I don't know what to say.

DANIEL: You don't have to.

He turns the lock control. The responsive encounter dissolves into the chosen interval. Alice's new knowledge of this farewell does not follow the fixed replay.

Daylight. Daniel is twenty-one. Ruth calls from the shop. Jonah has bought the wrong drinks. Alice is trying to free her sleeve from a gate latch.

ALICE: A hand would be useful.

Daniel's recorded younger body helps her. Their contact is warm and ordinary. His older awareness receives it.

The first passage through selected days is beautiful without being perfect: a missed bus, an argument over borrowed equipment, Alice falling asleep before a recording finishes, Ruth refusing to open on a Sunday.

The summer reaches its boundary.

Cut to daylight. The same angle.

ALICE: A hand would be useful.

Daniel's recorded body moves. His awareness has already anticipated the line.

The glass falls.

JONAH: Oh, come on.

At the next iteration, the player may press to reach for it. The gesture registers as Daniel's intention, but the fixed body's motion follows its recorded path. This limitation was previewed before the decision; it is not a surprise punishment.

The glass falls.

On a later afternoon, Alice turns to him.

ALICE: What are you looking at?

His recorded younger self answers.

YOUNG DANIEL: You.

The player hears the answer for the third time. His eyes cannot carry the years nobody else remembers.

The camera stays in the warm afternoon long enough for its continuation to become uncomfortable. Fade before a visible counter makes eternity into a score.

End title: ONE MORE SUMMER.

A quiet credits screen offers return to the pre-choice checkpoint. No celebratory achievement appears.

### C7S4 / E2 — Until the Water Stills

Daniel dismisses the fixed summer. The live encounter remains.

DANIEL: Not that.

ALICE: Then go back.

DANIEL: A little longer.

She looks at the return indicator, then him.

ALICE: Daniel.

DANIEL: I heard you.

His previously confirmed choice now takes effect; this is an authored action, not a second decision after commitment. There is no button-mashing struggle or countdown.

The ordinary shore begins to arrive in the wrong places: the work light through a reflected tree, cold against his palm, a tremor that belongs to his body elsewhere.

The small mark at his side darkens. Alice sees it.

ALICE: Is that from now?

DANIEL: Before I came in.

She looks toward the real position of his body. The separation is failing.

Without panic or a speech, she steps into the shallows. Her first attempt to take his hand passes through. She adjusts to the place where his sensation has arrived, and this time their fingers meet.
