# What the Water Keeps — narrative and performance guide
Version 1.4 · 22 September 2026 · Development direction, not recorded or playtested material

This guide turns the [story bible](01-story-bible.md) and two screenplay files into rehearsal, editing, and implementation decisions. It assumes a solo developer testing with readable dialogue, held illustrations, reused views, and temporary sound. Full voice acting, facial animation, and elaborate cinematics are later scope decisions. The story must work before those expenses.

## The relationship the player should experience

Daniel is worth knowing before he is worth rescuing. His attention makes people comfortable: he moves a hot cup, protects a recorder from rain, and carries the right spare part. The same ability becomes costly when completing a repair feels easier than answering the person waiting for it. Play both facts. Do not retrofit an ominous motive into every childhood notebook entry.

Alice enjoys him. Her decision to stay near him needs to be visible without a speech about his goodness. She swaps her larger piece of cake for his smaller one, lets an embarrassing recording play, and knows when his literal answer is a joke. They remain friends throughout their lived past. Their attraction is visible in chosen company and the occasional risk of asking for more time, but neither dating, a kiss nor a mutual confession occurs. She also wants time alone and work that he cannot share completely. Her departure does not turn a friend's career into a test of romantic loyalty.

Their repair after the argument is specific and provisional. They apologize for withholding news and assuming availability, then ask before arranging work or visits. Alice is considering her own room near work; Daniel helps with her folding recording table. No shared lease, trial month or romantic agreement exists. The player should be able to imagine them having another difficult conversation if Alice had lived. They have repaired how they treat a friend while leaving a more vulnerable question unanswered.

The reunion restores their ability to address each other. It does not restore the years between them. Daniel is thirty-four; Alice has just come from a working morning at twenty-two. Neither should immediately acquire the other's emotional pace. His prepared questions collide with her need to understand what has happened. The first explicit mutual acknowledgment comes in C7S2 after the limits and her refusal of repeated awakening, before any branch choice. It changes what they can say honestly, not what the machine can do.

These are editorial tests, not instructions displayed to players. The interactions should remain concrete. The player mixes an announcement so listeners can hear it; the interface should not label that action “accept imperfection.”

## Keeping tension alive without exhausting the player

Carry a live question through a scene, then let the answer alter the next question. Tension can be wanting to be invited, fearing a project will fail in public, avoiding an overdue reply or wondering whether an impossible encounter can answer back. It does not require continuous danger, friction or sadness. A solved problem must stay solved long enough to feel good.

| Chapter | Main unresolved pressure | How it changes instead of repeating |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What can the receiver reach, and will these strangers choose each other's company? | A strange sound becomes an actionable procedure; a practical kindness becomes a reason to meet again |
| 2 | Will their private games become something other people want to hear? What has adult Daniel stopped answering? | Creative play finds an audience; the present reveals an absence and two people who still need him |
| 3 | Does choosing more time together mean the same thing to each friend? | They risk being seen and succeed publicly; breakfast presents concrete future plans without converting them into a couple |
| 4 | Can closeness survive work elsewhere when neither asks the difficult question plainly? | The job creates specific hurt; asking before commitments repairs friendship while attraction remains unspoken |
| 5 | What can Daniel do when a day cannot be repaired? | Loss cannot be solved; present obligations and the source map let him take limited, useful actions |
| 6 | Can this encounter produce a new answer, and what will Alice need once it does? | Demonstrated success resolves the technical question; her shock and independent wants open the human one |
| 7 | Can they say what they avoided without turning honesty into a demand for more time? | A mutual admission resolves the romantic question before the player's irreversible decision; its truth does not remove the decision's costs |

Do not stage several versions of an almost-kiss. At one moment Daniel can choose a safe practical reply; later Alice can expose a little more by asking him to come; elsewhere their attention belongs completely to the recording or the argument. Change who takes the risk, what the other person thinks was asked, and what small action follows. Nobody should sound deliberately obtuse merely to prolong the plot.

Give subtext an observable consequence. A held bag keeps Daniel in the room while he asks his question. Finishing a task before answering can hurt Alice; stopping the task can show attention. A chosen walk gives them time together without a romance label. A room measurement concerns Alice's room, not a concealed proposal. Use the existing task and framing; do not add a reaction meter, secret approval flags or compulsory inspection of meaningful objects.

Allow relief to be complete at the scale of the scene. The radio works; Mara hears Alice's film; the fundraiser succeeds; the friendship apology is accepted. The larger question remains because life has not answered it yet, not because the score immediately announces another threat. Pausing or reading slowly never worsens a relationship or changes an ending.

The [scene tension map](14-friendship-and-tension-plan.md) names the want and turn for each of the twenty-eight slots. In rehearsal, ask which question changed. If nothing changed, shorten the exchange or make the existing action matter; do not add a new disaster.

## Voice and behavior

Each exchange starts with something the speaker wants from the other person now: help, an answer, room to work, a reason to stay, or permission to leave. Give the listener a different immediate priority. Dialogue then changes the action or the relationship. A scene cannot survive solely because its lines sound profound.

Use sharp lines sparingly. Surround a charged admission with ordinary speech and behavior; do not make every character trade polished metaphors. Let an interruption, an unfinished task, or a refused answer carry meaning. Ruth's practical impatience, Mara's exactness, Jonah's sociability, Alice's deflection and Daniel's literalness should remain distinguishable without speaker labels. During a table read, remove any impressive sentence that could be spoken unchanged by three of them.

Permit positive scenes to finish positively. The fundraiser's success does not require an immediate ominous sting; the audience needs time to share it. Save the largest change in scale for contact, where the player's work has earned it. Sadness is one movement among curiosity, comic embarrassment, pride, conflict, fear, relief, awe and ordinary care. The [revision notes](11-story-and-scene-revisions.md) map those movements to the existing scene slots.

**Daniel:** begin with the object or fact he can manage. Under pressure he finishes positioning a part before answering. Let pauses happen where a useful answer fails him. Avoid making every short sentence a tortured whisper. “It works” can be pride, defensiveness, or a plain report depending on who asked. At thirty-four, retain traces of the boy's delight in a successful repair.

**Alice:** vary her protection. Sometimes she jokes; sometimes she changes the subject; sometimes she answers directly and wishes she had waited. Her professional confidence is specific: she can judge a recording while misreading the travel plan. “Give me a minute” at reunion is a real request for space. Do not perform it as a beautiful farewell she already understands.

**Ruth:** speak while doing a task that matters to her. She can enjoy the children and still want her counter back. Her most useful authority is unperformed: a waiting hand, a look at the wrong screwdriver, a cup poured before an answer. Do not give her a wise maxim every time she enters.

**Jonah:** he is competent. His repair improves the machine, and his correction of the reversed guide plate matters. The customer calls and bedtime are concrete reasons to leave. His care continues after the boundary; it does not cancel the boundary. Avoid delivering “Don't do a live connection alone” with prophetic certainty.

**Mara:** precision is different from Alice's display. She asks for the recorder because it belongs to her and she wants to use it. Recording their mother's account of a move gives her a current project. She is allowed to enjoy a funny file without ending her disagreement with Daniel. Do not turn every object she picks up into evidence for the prosecution.

Reuse poses and prop states when testing these behaviors. Stage directions describe meaning; they are not all requirements for custom animation. A held view, a line break, and the removal of unnecessary dialogue can establish hesitation more cheaply than a close-up performance.

## Playing the revised middle

In C2S1, Mara stops writing to hear Alice's version; perform curiosity rather than a teacher approving an exercise. All sound orders are valid. In C3S2, Daniel's admission should arrive without a polished defense: the written date was a mistake, but he kept choosing another repair before returning the recorder. Mara can enjoy the old clip and still require her property back.

Let Alice's new job be exciting before it becomes a source of hurt. Her route choice expresses a practical preference; Mara's offer to take a late call supplies support without pretending the first day must go badly. In C4S3, holding the bag is useful, but it cannot settle the hall bookings or the four days of silence. Neither first approach is an apology selected by the interface. Preserve a little cooperation while the argument remains unresolved.

These scenes widen emotional contrast with existing people and objects. Do not foreshadow death over every happy beat. The [prepared table-read pack](13-paper-playtest-pack.md) supplies questions for testing what readers actually understand and feel.

## How the final encounter should breathe

Protect five steps in order: recognition; learning about the death; renewed conversation; understanding the encounter's limit; the late mutual acknowledgment. Do not intercut spectacle over Alice absorbing the news. Her reflex to think about sending work files is an ordinary mind trying to continue its day. Daniel does not resolve that loss by claiming he finished everything for her.

The private bus-plan detail supports her independence because the player saw Alice and Mara without Daniel. It is not a password or conclusive argument about consciousness. Her initiative, humor, refusal, and uncertainty must also feel like another person. The fiction defines her as conscious; performance makes the premise worth caring about.

The reset exchange has an exact information order. This encounter stops when Daniel leaves. Reactivation starts from the final morning. Alice would learn about her death again. She does not want that. Daniel acknowledges her preference. Only then do they address the whole archive, which includes other people's traces. Alice's preference about herself does not make her owner of everyone else's past.

The empty-place sequence needs silence from the characters. Hold the present plank, tree, and rise without overlaying an earlier pose. Accessibility descriptions identify what is visible; they should not add ghosts or interpret what Daniel has learned. Let the player turn without a skill check. Keep the late admission outside those three silent views; their absence does not need a romantic voice-over.

The late admission must sound like a risk taken now. Avoid a retrospective speech claiming that every childhood kindness was secretly courtship. Neither needs to prove that they would have lasted or that all those years had one inevitable destination. Alice can answer for her own feeling; Daniel can hear it without asking her to reward the machine or the years he spent building it. Leave a beat for the answer to arrive before introducing the final decision.

Tenderness is present before every ending: shared laughter, an attempted touch from both sides, attention to each other's uncertainty, and the same explicit mutual acknowledgment. The stable boundary remains intact: no kiss or hand contact arrives as a bonus for honesty. The fatal ending's successful hand contact is a consequence of the boundary failing, not the sole access to Alice's affection. Its outside-world aftermath remains necessary. The repeating summer contains an earlier friendship; its Alice cannot remember this new conversation or become a different partner on a later cycle. Morning remains costly because she stays gone and the archive is permanently lost.

## Optional material and duration discipline

Version 1.1 adds two optional, fully written beats inside existing scenes: the tangled tape in C2S2 and Alice's bad workday in C4S4. They are single-use inspections, have no relationship score, grant no collectibles, and do not change later lines or endings. They require only existing characters, props, and views. A player who skips both still understands the relationship and every ending.

Reserve up to two minutes total for both as a provisional read-and-interact allowance. Measure it; do not advertise it. Required additions about the amplifier, Alice's shock, and reset understanding replace quip chains or repeated explanation elsewhere. Keep the existing chapter envelope as a hypothesis until an actual table read and interactive timing pass. Do not add every conceivable pause to the upper bound and call the result a measured runtime.

In a tight edit, cut repeated hints, duplicated acknowledgments, and decorative inspections before cutting Alice's independent viewpoints, the actual disagreement, the safe-return demonstration, or the next morning. Each supporting character still needs a present-tense concern. No environment or voiced role has been added for the new material; the amplifier customer is conveyed by a bell, indistinct greeting, and signed slip.

## Localization and accessible writing

Keep stable scene and puzzle IDs. The v1.4 friendship revision retires `c3s1_kiss` / `c3s1_hand` in favor of the local `c3s1_stay` / `c3s1_walk` variants; do not retain romantic choice labels in an implementation. Other retained choice IDs keep their defined meaning. Dialogue, speaker labels, subtitles, messages, notebook text, interface consequences, and audio descriptions need separate string entries. Stage directions never accidentally become spoken lines. Attach context to each string: speaker's age, addressee, current knowledge, whether it is recorded speech, and any repeated use.

“Just one more” must use one approved source line from C2S2 and its exact later reuse. Translators need both scenes together; do not independently translate the offer as an ominous new sentence. Likewise, keep the recorder inscription, service dates, and owner corrections legible in each locale. Date formatting may change, but the twelfth as intake and fourteenth as completion cannot swap.

Allow translated text to reflow and enlarge. Do not require matching an English waveform, hearing a pun, recognizing handwriting, or reading a disappearing message. A silent reader must receive the same proof of repetition and the same explicit ending consequences. The ten-o'clock collection and eleven-o'clock breakfast are story appointments, not live device alarms.

Preserve the English final sentence exactly. An approved translation should preserve its sequence of remembering, sleeping, waking, and continuing rather than borrowing a famous quotation. The “Later entry” label prevents its past tense from seeming prophetic. The title remains provisional and should not become embedded into puzzle solutions.

## Revision record and next evidence

Earlier passes cut habitual quip exchanges, made affection practical, and gave the reunion time for Alice's own loss. It repairs recorder-case and phone pickup continuity, plants the previously missing spoken “Just one more,” separates Alice's preference from archive destruction, and resolves Jonah's safety check explicitly. The hall collection provides a small promise that Morning actually honors. P05 now compares an old guide with Jonah's altered present repair, using the existing reflection mechanic.

The v1.4 revision replaces the early romance and shared-home plans with friendship, increasing unspoken attraction and one late mutual acknowledgment. Its central performance risk is an endless series of evasions; its counterweight is a friendship the player enjoys even before that acknowledgment.

Next, read C3S1, C3S4 and C4S3–C4S4 together, then C6S4 through each ending, with different readers. Ask when they believed the friendship was changing, whether either person had already made a romantic commitment, what was repaired by the apology, and what each person finally chose to say. Ask what they think resets and whether E1 carries Alice's new answer forward; it must not. Do not ask only whether they cried. Log contradictory interpretations and revise the responsible line or staging. A text prototype can validate comprehension; emotional pacing still needs observed play and, later, performance testing.
