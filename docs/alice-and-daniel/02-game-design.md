# What the Water Keeps — complete v1 game design

**Status:** implementation specification for the proposed game; no game build is implied. Read alongside the story bible, screenplay, and production plan. Where the earlier creative recommendations differ, this package is the current design.

## 1. The experience

A premium, authored 2.5D narrative adventure for phones, with identical content on a possible Steam edition. Seven chapters contain 28 shared scene slots; the final slot has three variants. Twelve substantial interactions connect observation to releasing something. **151–153 minutes is the revised allocation, not a validated prediction.** Position this draft at roughly **2.5–3 hours**, testing an envelope of **140–170 minutes**. The original 3–4-hour recommendation is a ceiling, not a mandate: expand only for meaningful authored play. Measure read-throughs and playtests; never pad thin scenes. Optional rereading, idle time, and ending replays are additional.

Daniel's care first helps others: he repairs, listens, measures, records, and turns up. Those actions gradually postpone an unfinished life. Alice is playable in exactly two scenes without him; her work and decisions exist independently of his account.

The recurring action is **observe → understand → release → act**. Releasing a reflection is necessary to work in the present. Ordinary interactions share this rhythm without being supernatural. There is no combat, traversal challenge, inventory combination lottery, relationship score, consumable hint currency, or collect-everything requirement.

Temporal structure belongs to the author. The player does not select a year and rewrite it. Present-night scenes advance linearly; childhood and relationship scenes are explicitly dated authored scenes. Entering a recorded lake impression is a separate, visually identified operation.

## 2. Interaction grammar and world rules

Explore one composed camera view at a time. Select generous hotspots to approach, inspect, speak, or use objects. Walking is automatic. Objectives describe immediate intentions: “Find where Ruth left the key.”

The reflection interface has three steps: choose an established shoreline anchor; adjust a large alignment control until landmarks correspond; press **Observe**. Passive impressions contain recorded events and cannot answer new questions. **Return** is always visible. A found fact remains in the ledger after returning. Observe-mode controls cannot move present objects.

Only the Mere's lakebed lattice has this property. Windows, puddles, bottled water, radio recordings, and Alice's ordinary recorder do not reveal time. P02, P08, P11, P12, and the final encounter use actual lake phenomena. Other puzzles use normal tools, evidence, and conversation.

Dormant traces are unconscious records. The machine activates Alice's authentic, responsive person-state, remembering through her morning shore visit at twenty-two. She knows neither her death nor the intervening years. New experience persists only within this encounter; the source stays read-only. Returning ends that conscious stream. Reactivation would restart the twenty-two-year-old state unaware of earlier encounters. Alice learns these limits and consents to ending repeated activation. Erasure removes possible reactivation, not an underwater population continuously living. A story scene establishes her independent responsiveness without making her pass a password test.

The ledger has **Now**, **People**, and **Observed** pages. Notes distinguish Daniel's interpretation from corroborated events. Corrections remain visibly dated; the player cannot erase Mara's disagreement. No exhaustive diary is necessary to proceed. Returning after an interruption offers a short factual recap, current era, immediate task, and last unlocked interaction.

Conversation options choose approach or a small disclosure. They can change two or three lines, a later callback, or a gesture. They cannot prevent Alice's death, cancel her job, manufacture consent, or unlock a morally superior ending. Major disagreements happen because of established character decisions.

## 4. Twelve substantial interactions

Assistance ladder: **hint 1** identifies evidence; **hint 2** explains its relationship; **show next step** performs the next action. Assistance never changes dialogue, achievements, endings, or completion credit. Offer it on request, without penalizing reading speed. Puzzle allocations below include concurrent character dialogue and sit inside scene totals; never add them again.

### P01 — The radio / C1S3 / ordinary / approximately 4 minutes

Inspect three ordinary symptoms: a battery test indicates charge, the speaker makes sound, and a loose battery contact interrupts it. Match the intermittent-contact symptom to a replacement contact from Ruth's labeled tray; place it and select Test. Other parts stay on the mat with Ruth's practical explanation, without damage or scolding. Hints narrow attention to the functioning speaker, then identify the loose contact. Tap-select/tap-place, numbered diagrams, and textual observations replace tiny dragging, electrical knowledge, or pitch judgment. Completion produces clear radio speech and a ledger entry. Alice asks the price; Ruth answers with a joke and asks her to return if it fails.

### P02 — The hidden catch / C1S4 / actual reflection / approximately 4 minutes

From shore, align the shop's loading doors with their reflected frame. Observe Ruth opening a cabinet: reach beneath its lip and release the catch. The scene labels **Recorded event — cannot interact**. Return to the shop and perform those actions beneath a later covering panel. Trying the handle gives resistance and keeps clues available. Hints identify the lip, then the sequence. An enlarged diagram and text event log duplicate the animation. Daniel retrieves the damping clamp and sets aside an unrelated customer envelope. Knowledge crosses time; objects do not.

### P03 — Three useful sounds / C2S1 / ordinary / approximately 4 minutes

As Alice, create spacecraft effects: a tray becomes the engine, wet glass becomes a planet, and shaken paper becomes rain. Name each take before recording, then order engine → planet → rain under Alice's title **SPACE FILM — NO FILM YET**. Actors wait for Record; no ambient timing window applies. Mismatched takes can be replayed or replaced freely. Hints connect the scene description to a source, then order the next take. Captioned waveforms identify sounds; automatic clean-take mode preserves conversation. Save the sequence. This is effects work, without an unbudgeted additional radio-drama voice cast.

### P04 — How far counts / C2S2 / ordinary / approximately 4 minutes

Jonah measures from the wrong starting marker. Compare the dock edge, tree notch, and measuring cord, then move the origin to the shared notch and lay numbered distance pegs. Each child's throw animation is triggered by a single press; the distances are authored. The solution is a consistent origin, not making Daniel win. Wrong peg placement snaps beside the cord for reconsideration. Hints cite Alice's complaint, then highlight the origin notch. Numerals and tactile descriptions replace color dependence; no aiming or motion controls. Record everyone's actual distances. End with Ruth waving from the rise and the trio choosing to go eat.

### P05 — Jonah's winch / C2S4 / ordinary / approximately 5 minutes

Use a simplified fictional cradle panel with five labeled state cards: inspect brake → take load → release transport catch → lower → reapply brake. Diagram arrows and Jonah's demonstration establish the sequence. Select cards; do not manipulate realistic winch hardware. The mechanism blocks an unsafe order before motion. Hints trace the diagram, then identify the next state. Large discrete controls, narration, and single-step assist replace holding or simultaneous input. Completion gives machine-bay access. Jonah's remaining time is dialogue context, never a countdown. The interaction is not real equipment instruction.

### P06 — An honest correction / C3S2 / ordinary / approximately 4 minutes

Compare Daniel's ledger “14 June / Alice / recorder,” the service slip “12 June / mic socket,” and the underside inscription “Mara. Red button. A.” Alice gave Mara the recorder when she upgraded at twenty-one; Mara lent it for repair at twenty-two. Daniel copied the old owner and logged completion instead of receipt. Pair **receipt date**, **owner**, and **repair** with their evidence; correct to “12 June / Mara / socket repair.” Mara's testimony is supplied directly. Wrong placement prompts clarification. Hints distinguish the two dates, then show a pairing. Transcriptions and linear comparison replace visual cards. The original entry remains with its correction; Daniel returns the recorder. Tone changes his apology, not restitution. Small factual errors do not disprove his love.

### P07 — Let people hear it / C3S3 / ordinary / approximately 4 minutes

Mix a fundraiser announcement using speech, recorded water, and workshop-tap rhythm. Speech stays audible; both supporting layers sit within broad indicated bands. Three stepped controls, a clarity meter, and descriptive captions replace precision sliders and audio-only judgments. No timed effect cue exists. Incorrect settings remain playable with concrete feedback. Hints identify the obscured phrase, then its control. **Readable mix** supplies the solution. Preserve the accidental mug crash that Alice likes. Completion plays the short announcement while others handle the fundraiser's work; they do not exist simply to applaud her.

### P08 — What can be undone / C4S1 / actual reflection / approximately 5 minutes

Daniel and Jonah create a harmless, localized shoreline event using a marked washer. First observe its archived movement and return. Second select the interval's marked start/end and preview finite replay. Third fold the matching carrier back through that event's source to erase the trace. The physical washer remains; observing again yields no recorded movement. A brief observer-link test makes Daniel notice Jonah's wrist tap late; Return immediately restores ordinary awareness. No responsive person is activated; replay contains no bound human consciousness.

The interface requires identifying **original object**, **stored event**, and **observer** before erasure. Wrong selection is blocked with an explanatory diagram. Hints trace those three layers and show the missing label. Captions and large step buttons reproduce every sound result. A reversible preview precedes a clearly stated irreversible test; the event is expendable and always erased in canon. Save flags separately for observation, replay, erasure, and restored sensation. These are instructional prerequisites, not hidden endings criteria.

### P09 — A job she wants / C4S2 / ordinary / approximately 4 minutes

As Alice, reconcile a seven-week sound-assistant offer starting nine days later with bus and lodging arrangements. Place travel the evening before the first call and allocate the stated advance to lodging; displayed amounts are authored to work. An impossible arrangement shows its specific conflict; no offer expires. Hints point to “arrive the evening before,” then place that card. Large-print summary and **arrange itinerary** assist are available. Alice accepts in every route. Choose whether she first admits excitement or worry to Mara; neither informs Daniel for her or removes her later delay.

### P10 — Whose plan / C4S3 / ordinary / approximately 4 minutes

Sort Alice's equipment, Daniel's tools, and their kettle into independent possessions and a shared/deferred decision. Daniel has booked three hall weekends involving Alice without asking. Mark these **needs agreement**, not **already decided**. Misclassification starts clarification; it never rewards deciding for her. Choose a response and finish packing after recognizing each commitment. The argument remains in every solution. Hints identify ownership, then the unagreed booking. List sorting and spoken/text labels replace object dragging. Save the response for reconciliation; apologies address both her delay and his assumption. No shared lease exists at twenty-one.

### P11 — Three places in one shore / C5S4 / actual reflections / approximately 6 minutes

Inspect impressions at dock, lakeside measuring tree, and rise. Each reveals a persistent landmark plus orientation: dock bolt/triangle, tree's metal tape marker and notch/square, rise's iron footing/circle. Align each landmark with its recorded orientation on the present map. These are shoreline impressions, not remote surveillance. Combine the solved sectors into a connected-source diagram. Wrong alignment identifies the mismatched landmark without destroying a trace. Hints identify the invariant object, then rotation. Discrete buttons, spoken status, text, and auto-align remove visual-spatial barriers. Any sector order works; save each individually. Completion unlocks preparation and rehearses the final shots' geography without showing them prematurely.

### P12 — A door with a way back / C6S3 / actual machine / approximately 5 minutes

Use three established layers: P11's connected source map; the latest stored Alice identified by the morning shore visit; and the return carrier calibrated in P08. Place each into its labeled channel, select the matching learned symbol sequence, then review **Contact ready / Return available / No history alteration**. This is the final synthesis, not a new scientific cipher. Wrong assignments remain unpowered and explain which layer is missing. Hints cite the originating scene and then supply the next placement. All inputs are discrete; no shaking, rhythm, balancing, waveform hearing, or real-time loss of stability. **Complete calibration** remains available. Completion activates the first responsive encounter, preserving the full solution for restart recovery. Erasure and replay are reviewed here but are not executed by solving the puzzle.

## 5. Final choice and branch behavior

The empty-place sequence is shared and unhurried. Use the established dock plank, measuring tree, and rise camera compositions. **No ghost silhouettes, superimposed memories, remembered voices, or explanatory score.** Environmental sound may remain. The childhood revelation belongs before the branch: thirteen-year-old Alice glimpsed this common encounter, not a selected ending. Do not ask players to solve that image.

The offer uses Daniel's previously heard “Just one more,” assembled by the lake rather than spoken by a personified villain. Three equally legible intents open a consequence preview. Previewing does not commit; Back restores the same choice screen.

| Intent | Required plain consequence before confirmation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **One more summer** | “Bind yourself to the last complete summer. Everyone else repeats. You remember each loop and cannot change its events.” | E1: fixed summer at twenty-one; Ruth and represented participants replay. Responsive reunion Alice ends. External history remains unchanged. Daniel's awareness persists by the fictional self-sustaining lock. |
| **Stay beyond return** | “Remain after the live connection fails. Daniel will die in the water. This does not promise another life together.” | E2: after explicit confirmation, bodily awareness leaks; old blood from the dressed scrape appears. Boundaries fail, allowing this encounter's first hand contact. They talk until the water stills. |
| **Return and close the lake** | “Return to your life and permanently erase the lake's stored records. Alice stays gone; future reactivation ends. Ordinary recordings remain.” | E3: double goodbye, restore bodily control, stand, erase the lattice's special order, disable the core. Ordinary water remains. Write observations, sleep, eat breakfast, make ordinary contact, then complete the final entry after those events. |

No timed decision, input-speed test, morality total, completion percentage, or prior dialogue blocks a branch. The story acknowledges that shorter future visits are theoretically possible; Daniel's closure is voluntary. The final choice expresses what he commits to now. A persistent menu pause suspends presentation even after E2 confirmation; real elapsed time cannot kill him.

E3 retains the user's final line verbatim: “The water remembered me first — but I remembered them longest, and then I went to bed, and then, in the morning, I went on.” Grief is not cured. No replacement romance appears.

## 6. State, branching, and interruption recovery

Use versioned structured state; IDs are stable content keys, never localized display strings.

```text
SaveV1 {
  schemaVersion: 1,
  sceneId: C1S1..C7S4,
  beatId: string,              // stable resumable authored beat
  presentationMode: authored | present | impression | encounter,
  puzzle: { P01..P12: { status, solvedSteps[], localState } },
  evidenceIds: set<string>,
  ledgerCorrections: set<string>,
  choices: { apologyTone, jobDisclosure, packingResponse },
  canonFlags: { recorderReturned, jonahCheckInAgreed,
    observedReturn, previewedReplay, erasedTestTrace,
    restoredSensation, sourceAligned, woundDressed, contactEstablished },
  encounter: { disclosedDeath, disclosedYears, disclosedResetLimits,
    aliceClosureConsent, visitedBeatIds[] },
  ending: { previewed: set<E1|E2|E3>, committed: null|E1|E2|E3,
    completed: set<E1|E2|E3> },
  appliedEffects: set<string>, // prevents repeated grants/corrections
  recapKey: string
}
```

Settings live separately so text size and assists survive chapter replay. Narrative graph:

```text
C1S1 → … → C6S3 → C6S4 → C7S1 → C7S2 → C7S3
                                          preview ↔ choice
                                              ↓ confirm
                                E1 OR E2 OR E3 → credits
```

All intermediate dialogue branches rejoin within their scene, except named callbacks. Required facts, Alice's knowledge of reset limits, and her consent occur on the shared route. Ending confirmation commits atomically with the next beat; a crash cannot save an ending while replaying its confirmation as an uncommitted choice.

Save after solved puzzle steps, completed conversations, scene transitions, and ending commit. Treat effects as transactions with unique IDs: returning the recorder must not duplicate inventory removal, dialogue, or ledger corrections. Keep a last-good checkpoint if a write is interrupted. On resume, reconstruct from saved state rather than replaying acquisition triggers. Audio can restart at the current spoken line; completed actions do not replay. C5S2 resumes at the nearest respectful shot boundary, with an option to continue after its noninteractive emergency sequence.

After credits, offer **Revisit final choice** from a preserved pre-choice snapshot and chapter replay in a separate slot. Replaying does not overwrite the first completed ending or insert knowledge into the responsive Alice encounter. Do not label one ending “true,” award points for choosing it, or display completion pressure during the first ending.

## 7. Controls, accessibility, and platform parity

Design for phone landscape with left/right hand layouts, scalable text, and controls clear of device cutouts. Tap-to-select and tap-to-place are the universal baseline; dragging is optional. A persistent pause/menu control is available outside transitions, and application interruption pauses presentation. No interaction requires tilt, microphone access, multitouch, long holds, or rapid taps.

For Steam, map the same actions to pointer, keyboard focus, and controller navigation; controller prompts must describe focus and confirmation without pretending a cursor drag is necessary. Content, hint access, and endings remain identical. Any platform-specific achievements are optional production work, never progression gates.

Provide adjustable subtitle size, speaker names, separate dialogue/music/effects levels, descriptive captions, high-contrast focus, reduced motion, flash reduction, and optional haptics. Every sound-critical clue has a textual equivalent. Every color distinction includes shape/text. Every alignment has a discrete-input or automatic equivalent. Provide a dialogue history with era and speaker context, excluding future spoilers. Assess assistive-technology navigation during prototype testing rather than advertising unverified full support.

Before starting, offer a concise content note for bereavement, sudden medical emergency, and a death in water. Allow the emergency depiction to be shortened to immediate help and aftermath. This option changes presentation only. Selecting assistance or shortened presentation never marks the save as lesser.

## 9. Acceptance tests and review gates

1. **Duration:** measure read-throughs, then first plays with varied puzzle familiarity and assists. Confirm puzzle time is included. Investigate paths outside 140–170 minutes; revise positioning before padding dialogue, silence, traversal, or repetition.
2. **Comprehension:** after C2S3, players can say Alice died twelve years ago. After P08, they distinguish object, trace, replay, and erasure. Before confirmation, they can explain each ending's consequence in their own words.
3. **Agency:** run every dialogue combination affecting callbacks. All twelve interactions and all three endings remain reachable; no branch can save Alice or secretly invalidate affection.
4. **Ontology:** audit supernatural shots against shore contact, stored information, and established operations. Ordinary glass and recordings have no powers. Verify read-only source/reset dialogue and Alice's informed consent; no line implies dormant records are continuously conscious.
5. **Persistence:** interrupt before/after each puzzle step, recorder return, source-sector completion, contact disclosure, and ending commit. Resume without lost solved steps, repeated effects, duplicated items, or contradictory Alice knowledge.
6. **Accessibility:** finish with muted audio, reduced motion, enlarged text, discrete controls, and every assist enabled. Separately complete by touch, keyboard, and controller. No essential label clips or depends solely on color.
7. **Final choice:** pause each preview and E2 confirmation indefinitely. No narrative timer advances. Back never commits. E1 does not imply physical resurrection; E2 does not promise reunion after death; E3 preserves ordinary water and recordings.
8. **Emotional framing:** review the emergency and fatal ending with appropriate specialist/sensitivity input. Confirm there is no rescue-performance score, surprise medical clue, romantic reward for bodily death, or implication grief is a personal failure.
9. **Empty places:** frame-by-frame and audio-stem review confirms no figures, flashback overlays, voice fragments, or score in the three final empty shots.
10. **Content lock:** verify exactly two Alice-perspective scenes, twelve puzzle IDs, twenty-eight shared slots, six location kits, the dressed scrape's continuity, and the unchanged final written line. Log intentional changes across all package documents before implementation.
