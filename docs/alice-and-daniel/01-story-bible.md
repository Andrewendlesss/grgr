# What the Water Keeps — story bible
Version 1.3 · 22 September 2026 · Full narrative spoilers

This is the authored story baseline for the first playable draft, revised for character behavior, contemporary obligations, and the final encounter. It supersedes the exploratory alternatives in the original recommendations. The title is provisional; the characters, rules, chronology, and ending distinctions below are fixed for this draft. Production estimates remain estimates. The [narrative and performance guide](05-narrative-and-performance.md) translates this bible into playable beats and revision tests.

## The game in one paragraph

Daniel Vale returns to the lakeside where he met Alice Mercer at thirteen. Now thirty-four, he has built an instrument that can make the lake's preserved impressions respond. Over a single night, the player prepares the machine while experiencing their shared life in three interwoven strands. Alice died twelve years earlier, during an ordinary lunch he chose to spend with her. The apparatus succeeds: the woman in the water can surprise him, disagree with him, and remember things he never knew. Reaching her leaves him with a decision his engineering cannot make. He can bind himself to an unchanging summer, remain with her until his body fails, or end the lake's power and return to people whose lives still have a tomorrow.

## The emotional argument

The v1.2 screenplay pass preserves the facts below while widening how the story feels. C3S3 gives the group a sustained, earned success; C4S3 makes the practical disagreement hurt before reconciliation repairs anything; C5S1 makes Jonah's boundary specific; C6S3 makes preparation culminate in visible success; C6S4 gives Alice initiative and time to absorb the news. C7S1 asks about the future they had agreed to try, rather than pretending their reconciliation never happened. See the [scene revision record](11-story-and-scene-revisions.md).

The v1.3 pass lets the player cause the opening discrepancy and gives the middle interactions more expression and investigation. It changes no supernatural rule or ending. Alice accepts the job nine days before first call; the argument is four days after acceptance; either itinerary arrives the evening before work begins. The private timetable correction occurs before choosing a route, so the reunion memory remains available on both paths and unknown to Daniel.

Protect pleasure as part of the relationship's truth. A repaired radio can delight them. A good recording can sound good. A joke can remain funny without immediately becoming a clue. Technical success can be magnificent even when it cannot solve the human problem that follows. The melancholy comes partly from losing a life the player enjoyed participating in.

The central distinction is between loving someone and controlling the conditions under which they remain available.

Daniel's attention is a virtue before it becomes a refuge. His notebook contains useful measurements, promises kept, names remembered, and small acts of consideration. The game must never suggest that keeping photographs, writing about the dead, or grieving for years is a failure. His problem is the narrowing of his actions: he increasingly permits the past to claim every present obligation.

Alice's corresponding difficulty is avoiding vulnerable conversations until other people have already been hurt. She does not embody the correct philosophy. Their relationship works when both interrupt their own habits.

The ending asks for participation in an unfinished life. It does not promise closure as a permanent emotional condition. Daniel may miss her intensely the next day, and he still has to buy food.

Secondary themes are the fallibility of testimony, the work inside ordinary affection, unequal ownership of a shared past, and the difference between a pattern that repeats and a person who can change.

## Setting and visual truth

Greyford is a fictional town beside the Mere, a lake bordered by former light industry, repair yards, houses, and a public footpath. The town is economically ordinary and still occupied. There are bins on collection day, deliveries outside the shop, a bus that sometimes arrives early, and people Daniel does not know.

Use a temperate English-speaking setting without naming a real jurisdiction in the script. Calendar years remain unspecified. Technology ages through the story: Alice's first recorder is battered; her professional equipment is practical; present-day phones look functional rather than branded.

Six environment kits contain the game: dock and shore; Ruth's repair shop; the flat above it; the boathouse and machine; the measuring tree, path and rise; the street and bus shelter. Repeated places carry the scale of twenty-one years. Interiors seen in supernatural reflections must be visible from the shore; a bathroom mirror elsewhere cannot access the lake.

The lake is not photographed as evil. On many visits it is inconvenient, windy, brown at the edges, and entirely uneventful.

The present town must contain work that is still being done. A repaired hall amplifier awaits collection, a shelf needs fixing, Jonah has promised to be home, and the next bus carries people whose lives the story does not explain. Use existing props, background sound, and messages for this life. Do not add a town-exploration layer or new speaking cast to establish it.

## Characters

### Daniel Vale

**Ages:** thirteen, eighteen, twenty-one, twenty-two, thirty-four.  
**Work:** repair technician who gradually specializes in instruments and signal equipment.  
**Immediate want:** obtain a stable responsive encounter with Alice.  
**Deeper need:** participate in relationships he cannot preserve on his terms.

Daniel listens closely, enjoys identifying obscure faults, and can be quietly ridiculous. He remembers Jonah's interview date and carries the appropriate spare part when no one else thinks to. He dislikes being publicly thanked. His humor is literal and delayed: the joke often arrives after the conversation seems to have moved on.

His ledger begins as a way to remember repairs and promises. By thirty-four it is an attempt to eliminate uncertainty. He can honestly believe an entry that is incomplete or wrongly attributed. He is not deliberately fabricating an ideal woman; he gradually discovers the inadequacy of his account.

He can apologize without eloquence. His final growth is demonstrated through actions: returning the recorder, accepting Jonah's boundary, ending access to the lake, answering a message, and resting. No new romantic partner appears as a reward.

**Performance:** avoid permanent whispering and unbroken anguish. Adult Daniel can be brisk, annoyed, focused, even pleased when an experiment succeeds. The laugh he shares with Alice at the reunion matters.

**Physical habit:** he squares an object to a bench edge before beginning, keeps a thumb against the place in a notebook while listening, and checks someone else's grip before releasing a shared load. These are useful habits, not diagnostic symptoms. Under pressure he keeps finishing the small task in front of him while the actual conversation waits. At reunion, his first response to Alice sitting down is to look for somewhere comfortable for her; the impulse reaches a problem he cannot repair.

### Alice Mercer

**Ages:** the same as Daniel until her death at twenty-two; the activated trace remains twenty-two.  
**Work:** aspiring then employed location sound recordist.  
**Immediate wants during life:** do work she is proud of, leave town without making leaving a betrayal, and be able to return without being treated as someone who failed to escape.  
**At reunion:** understand what happened, have an actual conversation with Daniel, and see what remains available to him.

Alice is dry, observant, competitive about trivial things, and less organized than she claims. She makes terrible radio dramas before becoming good at practical recording. She likes noises other people want removed: a dropped tray, a bus door, someone laughing before the intended take.

Her defensiveness sometimes makes her unkind. She hides good news when discussing it might expose how much she wants it. A direct apology should cost her a little but remain possible. She does not answer every serious question with a perfect metaphor.

Give her physical affection, appetite, fatigue, ambition, and boredom. She sometimes wants to be alone without this foreshadowing death. Her two viewpoint scenes let the player know something Daniel does not.

The lake's vision happens to Alice. It does not determine her profession, secretly explain every joke, or make her a supernatural creation. She never spends nine years knowingly waiting to die.

**Performance:** ease before mystique. A brief honest answer may escape between jokes; do not turn this into a fixed dialogue formula.

**Physical habit:** Alice winds a loose cable around her fingers while considering an answer, then stops when it begins to twist. When absorbed in sound she raises one finger to ask for a moment, rather than explaining what she is listening to. With people she trusts she gives things to them without ceremony: half a carton, the end of a tape, her cold hand. At the reunion she initially reaches for equipment she no longer has. Her profession remains part of the life she has just lost.

### Ruth Vale

Daniel's mother runs the repair shop and wants to retire before her work becomes the whole of her life. She is affectionate and has limited patience for customers who expect miracles for free. She can mend something almost by feel but keeps postponing one annoying cabinet repair.

She welcomes Alice without trying to adopt her. She likes Daniel's attentiveness and also asks him to stop keeping objects that belong to other people.

Ruth retires when Daniel is twenty-seven and dies when he is twenty-nine. Her retirement has ordinary independent pleasures; her death is acknowledged briefly, without a second climactic loss sequence. Her old wave from the rise is established in a scene where she is simply calling the children home.

**Physical habit:** Ruth holds out a hand for the next tool without looking, and notices immediately when someone hands her the wrong one. She finishes pouring a drink before answering an impertinent question. Neither gesture should become a repeated comic bit. Her authority comes from having a task and other people to get through the day.

### Jonah Reed

Daniel and Alice's childhood friend becomes a boat repairer. He is socially quicker than Daniel and enjoys an audience. As an adult, he has a partner and young child outside the principal cast.

Jonah helped with early practical components of the machine because he loves Daniel and is curious. His limit is specific: he will not keep covering Daniel's work or missing family commitments. His refusal is not a threat to abandon him. He still wants breakfast together.

He understands enough of the apparatus to ask useful questions, but is not the assistant whose only purpose is to receive scientific exposition.

**Present stake:** he has been receiving calls about Daniel's completed hall-amplifier repair. Covering the missing customer reply costs him time on his own work. His boundary covers this as well as the evening at home; he cannot become Daniel's perpetual front desk. **Physical habit:** he checks a repaired item under its actual weight and wipes only the fingers he needs before using his phone. His care is practical, competent, and occasionally impatient.

### Mara Mercer

Alice's sister is four years older. She works in local transport scheduling and has shifts, bills, and a dry sense of humor different from Alice's: less performance, more precision.

She knows Alice's early recordings and the unflattering circumstances of some of them. She can challenge Daniel's ownership of a shared history without having a superior theory of grief.

The old recorder originally belonged to Alice, who gave it to Mara when upgrading at twenty-one. Mara brought it to Daniel for repair at twenty-two; he retained it after Alice died. Its return is a small concrete act, not an exchange that earns access to a better ending. Mara's own grief includes long stretches in which she is interested in other things.

**Present stake:** she wants to record their mother's account of an earlier move; their mother corrects Mara's version of the boxes and travel. This is an ordinary family project already underway, not a warning that another death is imminent. Mara has work in the morning and a shelf to install on Sunday. **Physical habit:** she makes room for someone else's cup before opening papers, and checks the time once she has agreed how long she can stay. She does not keep checking while someone is trying to answer her.

## What the player learns, and when

The current night proceeds forward. The other scenes are deliberate authored cuts, not evidence that Daniel can roam freely through time.

Chapter 1 introduces the machine as a repeatable physical procedure and gives the dock meeting room to breathe. Chapter 2 reveals that Alice died twelve years earlier. Her death is not a late twist or a fact hidden by unnatural dialogue.

Chapter 3 establishes romance, an incomplete ledger, and an ordinary breakfast from the last day. Chapter 4 develops Alice's independent choice and the machinery's three operations. Chapter 5 presents the death and the present costs of Daniel's fixation. Chapter 6 proves the machine works and clarifies what Alice knows. Chapter 7 brings the opening reflection and the reunion together before offering the endings.

The main reversal changes the meaning of Alice's childhood tears. A second, quieter discovery changes the meaning of Daniel's notebook: even an honest record is a perspective.

## Lake and machine: authoritative fiction rules

These rules describe an invented phenomenon. They are not claims about physics or consciousness.

### 1. The source is local

An unusual order within the lakebed stores shoreline events and the states of people during contact with that place. A person-state includes that person's memories at the time, even memories of elsewhere. It does not grant access to strangers who never came to the Mere.

Only surfaces of this lake reveal the phenomenon. A device can select and stabilize the connection, but ordinary water removed from the site has no continuing power. All reflections used in play have a geographically established shore connection.

### 2. Storage and access do not follow human time order

A rare unstable view can connect with another point in the source's history. Alice's first glimpse is a view into the common reunion scene before any ending branch. Nothing in that glimpse names or guarantees Daniel's final decision.

History remains self-consistent: the glimpse was always part of their meeting. Destroying the source later does not erase past occasions on which it functioned. There is no unrestricted predictive device; Daniel can stabilize anchored traces, not choose arbitrary future dates.

### 3. A stone interrupts an image, not a person's ordinary memory

The childhood glimpse is brief, overwhelming, and not consolidated into a coherent account. Breaking the surface ends access. Alice retains unease, a remembered sound, and familiarity without the sequence.

This cannot be used later to erase inconvenient arguments, consent, injuries, or ordinary knowledge. The fact that she cannot narrate the vision does not mean she cannot remember meeting Daniel.

### 4. Passive observation and responsive encounter differ

Most gameplay accesses event impressions: actions play out and provide information. They cannot answer a new question.

The completed apparatus activates a preserved person-state. The Alice it reaches has her own preferences, can generate new answers, and acquires new memories during their encounter. She is an authentic conscious continuation through this fictional medium, not a fantasy manufactured by Daniel. Her biological life ended twelve years earlier. Dormant traces are records, not conscious people waiting beneath the water.

The source records biological person-states at the shore; an activated impression has no biological body and cannot update its stored person-state. New memories persist only for the current session. Disconnect ends this active stream. A later activation would begin again with the same final-morning Alice, unaware of this conversation. The lake can store passive images of an encounter without turning the reflected participant into a newer reactivatable person-state. This distinction prevents an unnoticed route around the reset rule.

Her latest state dates to the final morning at the shore. She knows about the proposed flat and her job. She does not know what happened at lunch or what Daniel has done since.

The player receives evidence through Alice recognizing and extending a private exchange previously witnessed in her viewpoint scene and unknown to Daniel. It is not a password Daniel administers or a rigorous philosophical test of consciousness. The fiction establishes that she is a conscious continuation; the drama makes her independence credible through initiative, ordinary memories, uncertainty, and a preference that frustrates his hopes. Do not rely on a machine declaring her authenticity or one clever anecdote resolving all uncertainty for an audience.

### 5. An encounter does not restore the life outside it

Alice cannot walk out of the water into a new biological life. Objects do not transfer between eras. New conversation is possible within the connection, but its environment and embodied life cannot extend into an unwritten future.

Brief further visits are technically possible, but would reawaken the same Alice into the news of her death and the lost years. Daniel explicitly raises leaving and returning. Alice understands that she would not remember this encounter and states that she does not want that repeated awakening. She consents to this meeting ending without further activation. Daniel chooses permanence with that preference in view, rather than treating a person as a cure for his grief.

Preserve the order in the scene: Alice learns that the current encounter ends; she asks whether a later visit resumes it; Daniel answers that it starts from the final morning again; she states her preference; he acknowledges it without bargaining. She does not authorize the disposal of every other person's trace. Her own request and his decision about the entire source are distinct. The locked summer also does not carry forward her newly expressed preference: choosing it is Daniel's retreat into recorded behavior, and the story must not imply her blessing.

Erasure also destroys the source's other dormant records and their potential activation. The scene acknowledges this irreversible archive loss; it does not claim that destroying shared history is a universal treatment for bereavement. No continuously conscious population is being killed beneath the lake. The three endings remain personal, costly commitments rather than a menu of technical necessities.

### 6. Real time and bodily vulnerability persist

The live connection suppresses ordinary awareness of Daniel's body. A safe rehearsal demonstrates delayed sensation and successful return. The immersive apparatus extends into the shallows; staying after its safe stage leaves him unable to protect his body in the water.

Before connection, a bracket scrapes his side through his shirt. He dresses the minor wound. The later spreading mark is old blood becoming visible as physical sensations enter the encounter, not a newly revealed illness or blood payment. Its appearance warns of deteriorating separation; the injury alone does not kill him.

An attention or safety indicator belongs to the story's staged sequence. It is never a wall-clock deadline imposed on the player.

### 7. The three operations are separate

**Observe and return:** select a trace, experience it, release it, regain bodily awareness. Demonstrated repeatedly.

**Lock an interval:** a finite sequence becomes a self-sustaining recurrence. The full bargain binds Daniel's continuing awareness into that interval. His living body is not rescued; the lake sustains the bound awareness after the body's live connection cannot continue. Other participants repeat their recorded states. This is why the loop can last while the fatal live encounter cannot.

**Erase at source:** a previously demonstrated operation removes a localized trace irreversibly. The final machine connects the source as a whole, enabling the corresponding global act. It destroys the information-bearing order that makes further encounters possible. Breaking the apparatus afterward prevents its immediate reuse; building another cannot replace the lost source.

The marked-washer test demonstrates the operations on a harmless event. Nobody experiments by deleting a human consciousness as a tutorial.

### 8. Touch has a consistent boundary

Within the stable reunion, Daniel and Alice occupy different layers. An attempted ordinary touch does not meet. As the live connection fails, sensation and position bleed across the separation; the final contact in the fatal branch becomes possible.

This rule is demonstrated visually in a safe rehearsal, not explained in the last breath. “First and only time” refers to this reunion. They touched and held one another during life.

### 9. The lake is not a conversational villain

No caretaker explains its origin. No intelligence delivers a manifesto about human attachment. Its offer uses Daniel's familiar phrase, “Just one more,” and a visible replay he can understand.

The work can leave the source's origin unexplained while being exact about its behavior.

## Ending contracts

### E1 — One More Summer

The interval is the last complete summer before Alice's death, when both leads are twenty-one and Ruth is alive. The loop closes before autumn. All people appear at their historical ages; no impossible gathering of incompatible versions is silently assembled.

Daniel's thirty-four-year-old awareness inhabits his younger self. The warm first iteration includes the small problems that existed then. On repetition, he anticipates a spilled drink and a familiar line. He cannot change the recorded action. He can remember the last cycle; no one can share that accumulated experience.

This ending grants continuation and company at the cost of mutual development. Outside history continues unchanged. Never imply that everyone has physically returned to Greyford.

### E2 — Until the Water Stills

Daniel refuses the loop and chooses to keep the temporary encounter open beyond safe return. The choice is explicit and confirmed. Pausing, silence, slow reading, or abandoning the app can never select it.

Alice does not invite him to die. She acknowledges the remaining route to shore. When he nonetheless stays, she does not punish him or withdraw tenderness. The stain appears; she enters the shallows and takes his hand. Their remaining conversation concerns something small they actually did together.

The water stills. A restrained real-world aftermath shows Jonah at the vacant apparatus and a practical rescue response, without bodily spectacle. There is no afterlife scene or celebratory reunion. Alice's care is not a prize available only through death.

### E3 — Morning

Daniel chooses return and permanent closure. The shared farewell has already supplied his goodbye to Alice; in this branch, he adds the second goodbye to the thirteen-year-old still waiting for the afternoon to continue. No child apparition is required.

He restores bodily control, stands in the real shallows, performs the already-established source-erasure action, and disables the core. No mass explosion, emptied reservoir, or damaged town is introduced. The supernatural lake is gone; ordinary water remains. This explicitly adopts the refinement proposed in the recommendations.

He collects his phone and food, then walks past the dock plank, tree, and rise. Their framing matches earlier scenes, but there are no figures, spectral voices, or memory overlays. At home, he checks in with Jonah and arranges breakfast at eleven, which explicitly replaces the eight-o'clock safety call. He begins an entry and goes to bed. The playable morning includes tea and the previously promised ten-o'clock amplifier collection, then leaving for breakfast. A later ledger entry supplies the final sentence retrospectively, so its past-tense account of sleeping and waking describes events that have already occurred.

Retain the supplied final sentence exactly as that later, final ledger entry:

> The water remembered me first — but I remembered them longest, and then I went to bed, and then, in the morning, I went on.

The next morning provides a brief playable coda. Tea, the unfinished ordinary day, and leaving the flat complete the action. His grief is neither cured nor invalidated.

## Dialogue and editorial rules

A scene needs an immediate task and a social imbalance. The dialogue should change that imbalance or reveal why it persists. Most lines should survive without knowing the game's theme.

Children talk concretely; adults have histories, interruptions, and unequal information. Alice is not always the cleverest person in the room. Mara and Ruth do not share her voice. Daniel sometimes asks a good question instead of delivering exposition.

Reserve lyrical language for the ledger and a few earned lines. Avoid repeated formulations about time, echoes, remembering, and letting go in ordinary conversation. Let a failed apology initially sound like a failed apology.

A performed read-through should check whether actors can understand what each person wants on every line. Any remaining lore explanation must be cut against physical action or evidence already available.

## Content treatment and factual boundary

The selected medical frame is sudden cardiac arrest with no cause established after investigation. The British Heart Foundation explains that unexplained sudden death following cardiac arrest may be recorded as sudden arrhythmic death syndrome. The script should not invent a particular inherited diagnosis or make Alice's death a result of the lake. [BHF: SADS](https://www.bhf.org.uk/informationsupport/conditions/sads)

Cardiac arrest can occur without warning; a credible emergency response matters even though this fictional attempt does not succeed. Depict Daniel seeking help promptly and responders acting, without a player-controlled resuscitation test. A clinical reader should review staging before animation and recording. [BHF: cardiac arrest](https://www.bhf.org.uk/cardiacarrest)

Offer concise optional content information for sudden bereavement and drowning. A reduced-intensity presentation can cut from the dropped utensil to the aftermath and from the fatal hand contact to the real-world coda. It must preserve comprehension and never change an ending's availability.

## Story acceptance criteria

The finished draft must pass these questions without outside explanation:

- Can a player explain what Alice wanted before she died?
- Does Daniel have attractive qualities independent of his suffering?
- Does every supporting character have something at stake in the present?
- Is Alice's death explicitly unrelated to Daniel's attendance or the lake?
- Does the childhood glimpse fit every ending?
- Can the player distinguish a passive replay, a responsive encounter, and the locked summer?
- Is the fatal branch a deliberate informed choice?
- Is leaving available before the bodily connection fails?
- Does the closure destroy future access without rewriting the past?
- Does the final morning contain an action that joins Daniel to living people?
